\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}
% =====================================================================

This section evaluates the system in the order the pipeline builds it. \Cref{sec:dataset} sets out
the shared setup---the human-annotated flaw benchmark and the recall metrics and matching protocol
every result is scored under. \Cref{sec:gen-experiments} takes the \emph{physics-guided generator}:
whether the geometric control governs the motion, when the trajectory commits, and how far the scene
library reaches. \Cref{sec:eval} takes the \emph{\PhyReAct{} critic}: its flaw recall on the
evaluation core, against the annotated reference and published evaluators, and its agreement with
human ratings. \Cref{sec:icl-exp} takes the \emph{context-training self-evolution} of the
critic---whether lessons distilled from recorded misses raise recall without touching a
weight---and \cref{sec:refine} closes the critic's outward channel, feeding a verdict back as a
prompt rewrite that regenerates the clip. The evaluations use distinct held-out settings that should
not be conflated; \cref{sec:eval-scope} sets them side by side.

\subsection{Experimental setup}\label{sec:dataset}
This setup---models, benchmark, and matching protocol---is shared by both systems; each system's own configuration accompanies its results.

\paragraph{Models and specialists.} The critic runs one served vision-language model for both its text-only planner and its video-aware semantic verifier---Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct~\cite{qwen3vl2025}, in BF16 without quantization---together with a frozen stack of low-level specialists, one per capability: SAM~3~\cite{sam3_2025}, TAPNext++~\cite{tapnext2025}, a depth model, PaddleOCR, and FlexSED~\cite{flexsed2025}, with a closed model as the fallback verifier. \Cref{tab:specialists} lists, for each capability, the backend that runs it and the object it returns, together with the exact checkpoint, size, and license; every component is frozen at the listed revision for all reported results.

\subsubsection{Benchmark}\label{sec:bench-spec}
The benchmark is a corpus of $1{,}500$ AI-generated clips in which a person watched each clip against its prompt and recorded every place the video failed to deliver what the text asked for; the generating model is not recorded, no clip is attributed to a specific system, and the human-written fields are the ground truth throughout. Each flaw is a localized record with four fields always present---the \textbf{violated fragment}, a free-text \textbf{rationale}, a \textbf{severity}, and the annotator's \textbf{confidence}---and two present when the annotator can supply them, a \textbf{time span} and a \textbf{box track}. Two coarse grouping tags, a \emph{modality} tag (seen, heard, or both) and a ten-item \emph{type} tag, are machine-assigned by majority of three zero-temperature model votes and never treated as ground truth. Per-field coverage, the per-type breakdown, and the timing distributions are given in \cref{sec:app-benchmark}.

Three recorded stages (\cref{fig:benchcomp} in \cref{sec:app-benchmark}) narrow the $1{,}500$ clips and $2{,}582$ flaws to the evaluation set: \emph{clip selection} keeps retrievable, dynamic clips, and \emph{flaw selection} keeps only the ``seen''- or ``both''-tagged flaws a pixel-only critic could find, leaving $1{,}107$. From these a deterministic seeded procedure, stratified by severity, flaw count, and duration, draws \emph{the frozen core} of $150$ clips and $306$ flaws (a $29$-clip continuity stratum exempt from replacement); a separate pool of $711$ clips is held disjoint from both frozen sets for learning. The core is an optimization set, so results on it measure fit to that core. One clip produced no measurement, so recall is reported over $149$ clips and their $304$ flaw entries, never the full $306$---the two excluded rather than counted as missed, making the reported rate very slightly optimistic.

Unlike clip-level resources\label{sec:bench-vs}---Physion-Eval~\cite{physioneval2026}, VideoPhy-2~\cite{videophy2_2025}, and per-dimension suites such as VBench~\cite{vbench2024} and T2V-CompBench~\cite{t2vcompbench2024}---our corpus attaches each failure to the exact prompt words with an optional time span and box track, so an allegation can be matched to a particular annotated failure rather than only correlated with a clip-level score. This localization does not improve reliability: our corpus is single-annotator and contains only flawed clips, whereas VideoPhy-2 provides redundant judgments and majority resolution.

\subsubsection{Reference standard and matching protocol}\label{sec:eval-protocol}
The evaluation core is the reference standard: its $149$ clips carry $304$ annotated flaws, the denominator for all core recall analyses. Every clip carries at least one flaw, so with no clean clips recall is directly measurable and precision is not---a detector that flags everything scores perfectly on recall alone---and results are reported alongside the rate at which accusations land on a real flaw. Recall is counted over flaw entries rather than distinct spans, since the matching protocol claims each entry at most once. Matching\label{sec:matching} is performed after critic inference and cannot affect the critic's outputs. The unit of comparison is a pair of sentences---what the annotator wrote and what the critic reports---judged on meaning as a \emph{whole} match (the same defect), a \emph{partial} match (the critic identifies at least one aspect of a compound annotated defect), or a non-match. Whole and partial pairings are the candidate edges for maximum bipartite matching in which each finding and each flaw is used at most once, after which each flaw is reported as whole, partial, or missed. The matching judge is distinct from all evaluated components, voting at temperature zero with a retry-and-refallback rule, and aborts rather than reporting a number if more than $2\%$ of flaws yield no usable outcome.

\subsection{The physics-guided generator}\label{sec:gen-experiments}

Three questions organize the generator experiments, each separate from the method definition:
whether the geometric control overrides prompt-implied motion (\cref{sec:gen-prompt}), when a
conditioned trajectory becomes insensitive to control withdrawal (\cref{sec:gen-release}), and how
the pipeline behaves beyond the single-body probes (\cref{sec:gen-breadth}). The first and third
analyses use the deployed generator; the release analysis is an explicitly separate mechanism probe
on the second backbone whose withdrawal operation is defined in \cref{sec:gen-withdrawal}.

\subsubsection{Setup: from simulation to a controllable clip}\label{sec:gen-setup}

The deployed backbone is Wan~2.2-VACE-Fun-14B, run through the \code{WanVACEPipeline} interface with
a fixed negative prompt, no appearance reference, and deterministic sampling given the recorded seed.
Scenes are simulated in MuJoCo and rendered to $81$ frames at $16$\,fps; because the simulated
duration varies by scene, this render clock stretches playback by $1.8\times$--$2.3\times$, so
trajectory shape and event order come from the simulation but absolute video speed does not equal
simulator time. Generation is staged: controls are rendered first, a 1.3B checkpoint screens each
scene once, and visibly degenerate outputs are not queued for the 14B model---a screening rule that
is part of the reported pipeline rather than a property of the generator. Surviving scenes are
generated in bf16 at $848\times480$ with the UniPC sampler at $50$ steps and guidance scale $5.0$,
conditioned on the control video (globally normalized MuJoCo depth, or a silhouette for predeclared
flat-scene templates), an all-white mask, and the text prompt; the conditioning scale is $s=1.0$ in
the deployed run and examined separately at $\{0.5,0.8,1.0\}$.

\subsubsection{Control fidelity under prompt variation}\label{sec:gen-prompt}

We fix the geometric control---one projectile-depth video---and vary the prompt: three prompts share subject, scene, camera, material and style, differing only in the motion clause: a strong horizontal traverse, a neutral one, or one adversarial to the control (drops nearly straight down, almost no sideways motion). Each runs under three regimes---no control, control over the first $10$ of $50$ steps, control throughout---at three seeds (partial), two (unconditioned), one (full). Horizontal travel (fraction of frame width) is tracked in the finished clip; metric and decision rules were registered before generation.

The two inputs cross over (\cref{fig:prompttraj}). Without a control the prompt sets the motion:
the clause-attributable range is $0.875$ against $0.252$ between seeds. The unconditioned clips
also differ in framing---a median tracked ball area of $8{,}846$ pixels against $211$ under the
control---so travel-fraction is not comparable across regimes, and within-regime comparisons carry
the argument. With the control throughout, the clause-attributable range falls to $0.001$; in
between, with the control over the first fifth of sampling then withdrawn, the two sources swap, the
clause range at $0.060$ against a seed range of $0.427$.

The trajectories locate where the shortfall arises (\cref{fig:prompttraj}). A clip conditioned over
the first ten steps tracks the control's arc through the flight and departs only at the landing,
stopping near $0.77$ of the frame while the control's ball rolls on to $0.99$: the lost travel is
the post-contact roll, not a different flight---the same phase \cref{sec:gen-release} finds most
sensitive to early withdrawal. Of the three clauses at this seed, two follow the control from the
first frames and the neutral one does not, staying a mean $0.243$ of the frame from the control's
path over the first ten frames. The control-set trajectory thus persists through the text-only steps.

\subsubsection{Trajectory commitment under control withdrawal}\label{sec:gen-release}

Moving the withdrawal point locates the noise level at which the simulated trajectory becomes fixed. The withdrawal operation, its second backbone, the noise-level coordinate $\sigma_{\mathrm{rel}}$, and the two variants that clear or keep the multistep solver's cached derivatives are defined in \cref{sec:gen-withdrawal} (\cref{eq:withdrawal-mask,eq:flow-sigma}); the scenes are a single moving rigid body, chosen for a closed-form trajectory (one of the three yields only five post-contact frames, so its windowed comparisons rest on two), each schedule setting at one seed. Withdrawing partway leaves the remaining steps unconditioned; since noise falls as sampling proceeds, a \emph{high} $\sigma_{\mathrm{rel}}$ is an \emph{early} withdrawal, so a clip that still follows the simulation after an early withdrawal shows the trajectory was fixed before the control was removed---and the earliest withdrawal that preserves agreement marks that point. Three runs each answer a different question: one fixes a withdrawal point and repeats it over $16$ seeds (seed variability), one sweeps six points on one seed (transition shape), and one re-scores a denser sweep with an independent tracker (measurement dependence).

\paragraph{Metrics.} Each run is paired against a $\sigma_{\mathrm{rel}}=0$ run sharing its
simulation, control, prompt, sampler, and seed, and both are tracked frame by frame (by colour,
except the third run's segmentation tracker). \emph{Retention} is the per-axis Pearson correlation
between generated and control paths (\cref{sec:app-retention}), scored separately over an
\emph{airborne} and an \emph{after-contact} window split at the control's ground-contact frame $t_c$
(\cref{eq:contact-frame}); near $1$ means the object moved as simulated, and the split localises
disagreements a whole-clip value hides. Two prefixed failure counts accompany it---a
\emph{whole-clip disagreement} (full-clip horizontal $<0.7$) and a \emph{late-window disagreement}
(whole-clip horizontal $<0.7$ while airborne horizontal $>0.9$).

\paragraph{Airborne and after-contact agreement under an early release.} The first run fixes the release point at $\sigma = 0.952$ and repeats two scenes over $16$ seeds each, in two variants (clearing or keeping the sampler's cached previous-step derivatives) against the never-released baseline: $96$ clips, $16$ per cell. Releasing at $\sigma = 0.952$ leaves the thrown ball's airborne flight intact, with per-seed horizontal agreement dropping by $0.003$ relative to never releasing ($95\%$ CI $0.001$ to $0.004$, $n=16$ paired seeds). Over the whole clip the same withdrawal reduces horizontal agreement by $0.13$ ($95\%$ CI $-0.02$ to $0.28$) and $0.18$ vertically ($95\%$ CI $0.07$ to $0.29$). The disturbance is thus confined to the motion after ground contact: scoring only post-contact frames, the generated ball's horizontal motion runs \emph{opposite} to the simulation's on $9$ of $16$ seeds at $\sigma = 0.952$ and on $0$ of $16$ when the control is never released. Whole-clip disagreements number $3$ of $16$ against $1$ of $16$, and late-window disagreements $2$ of $16$ against $0$ of $16$.

Sweeping six withdrawal points on one seed across three scenes gives retention against noise level (\cref{fig:injsweep}). For the thrown ball, $\sigma=0.952$ gives after-contact horizontal $-0.63$ (opposite to the simulation) with the airborne window still $1.00$, and $\sigma=0.903$ or later restores after-contact to $0.98{+}$ through the latest release, $\sigma=0.555$. A dropped ball stays above threshold everywhere: after-contact horizontal is $1.00$ throughout, and vertical---the informative axis---is lowest at $0.78$ ($\sigma=0.952$), rising to $0.99{+}$ from $\sigma=0.903$. A ball off a ramp stays at $0.99{+}$ in both axes. A third run---three thrown-ball points at three seeds and two guidance settings, $18$ clips, segmentation tracker---reproduces the direction: airborne horizontal $1.00$ (mean over $6$ clips at $\sigma=0.952$, $95\%$ CI $0.998$--$1.000$) while after-contact horizontal averages $0.69$ over an interval spanning zero ($-0.04$ to $1.43$, $n=6$), recovering to $0.99$ and $1.00$ at $\sigma=0.903$ and $0.833$; at the latest point the tracker finds no object on $3$ of $6$ clips (low-contrast ball on grass), excluded, widening that interval.

Across the runs the trajectory stops responding to control removal in a narrow band, $\sigma \approx 0.94$--$0.96$. Under \cref{eq:flow-sigma} at $S=40$, $\kappa=5.0$ this sits in the high-noise portion of sampling, consistent with the critical-window account~\cite{phasetrans2024,dynregimes2024,critwindows2024} and above the backbone's expert switch at timestep $0.875\,N$~\cite{wan22dual2026}, so motion is fixed before the handoff; we report it in noise level rather than step index because a change in $S$ or $\kappa$ moves the step but not the level~\cite{sdedit2022,p2train2022}. The two components do not fix together: on the thrown ball, $\sigma=0.9522$ drops after-contact agreement below threshold in both coordinates ($-0.632$ horizontal, $+0.016$ vertical), and one point later ($\sigma=0.9025$) the horizontal recovers to $+0.981$ while the vertical is still $+0.260$, not reaching $+0.894$ until $\sigma=0.8331$; the dropped ball shows the same ordering, so a schedule set by the horizontal alone would be too early. (Over the whole clip the ordering reverses---vertical $+0.986$ against horizontal $+0.421$---because the large flight excursion dominates the correlation and hides the rebound.)

The failure is specific: the flight is reproduced, the post-contact behaviour is not. Contacts are a documented failure mode of video generators~\cite{videophy2024}, traced to a weak prior over contact; where the prior is weak the control must stay present to override it, so applied throughout it reproduces the contact on every seed and the reversal appears only under early withdrawal. Clearing the sampler's cached derivatives changes the whole-clip result by at most $0.04$ horizontally ($95\%$ CI $-0.04$ to $0.13$, $n=16$), the largest per-seed difference measured on any window or direction.

\subsubsection{Scene-library breadth and failure boundaries}\label{sec:gen-breadth}

The same authoring, simulation and conditioning path scales beyond the single-body probes, producing $1{,}314$ scenes across $66$ \emph{kinds} (a kind is a template, a scene one parameter setting, simulated and generated once); $591$ of $652$ simulations passed a pre-render inspection of simulator output, not generated clips, judged to show the intended behaviour clearly enough to generate from. \Cref{fig:scenebreadth} shows twelve kinds as generated frames at body counts up to $34$, and \cref{fig:scenepairs} three as depth control and generated clip at matched timestamps.

Fidelity does not extend uniformly. Assessed by manual inspection with the colour tracker of \cref{sec:gen-release}, single-object scenes reproduce the simulated trajectory throughout (\cref{fig:gencases}), but a multi-object interaction such as a billiards break does not---neither the deployed backbone nor the alternates produced a collision in which every body moved as specified---and its commitment point is unmeasured, the colour tracker being unable to follow a scatter as one path. The same rigid-body engine produces every scene, deformable and granular kinds built from its particle-and-link primitives (springs, linked segments, contacting bodies, buoyancy), so the library excludes fluid, smoke and finite-element soft-body solves.

\subsection{The \PhyReAct{} critic}\label{sec:eval}
The benchmark and metrics are set out in \cref{sec:dataset}; this subsection reports the critic's recall on the evaluation core and breaks it down by clip property and specialist trace. How all the settings in this section should be read together is deferred to \cref{sec:eval-scope}.

\paragraph{Implementation and cost.}
The served Qwen3-VL model (\cref{sec:dataset}) runs with tensor parallelism across two GPUs; planner
and verifier are separate calls, the planner seeing the prompt and claim state but no frames and the
verifier seeing densely decoded, timestamped frames and one declared predicate. Every reported
measurement uses the logged dense-input path with the frozen target, BF16 inference, and pinned tool
revisions. Because a detector, a tracker, and a full vision-language judgement differ in cost, cost
is reported as model and tool calls per clip rather than wall-clock time, and uncertainty by
resampling whole clips, since two flaws in one clip are not independent.

\paragraph{Planner validity.}\label{sec:planner-gaps}
Inspected before end-to-end evaluation without video or tool access, the prompt-only planner
produced a valid, compilable plan for all $233$ claims of $22$ annotated clips (one or two calls per
clip); $27$ claims ($11.6\%$) carried a specialist action that survived validation, against $2.3\%$
under an earlier tabular format. On diagnostic sets requiring quantitative measurement the routing
rate was $19/30$ across counting, spatial, and ordering claims and $10/10$ for synthetic counting
prompts---tests of routing and syntax, not verdict accuracy. The supported vocabulary lacks an ``on
top of'' predicate and rejects negated measurements; such claims fall back to the generalist
verifier.

\subsubsection{Flaw recall on the evaluation core}
\label{sec:headline}
Over the $149$ core clips and their $304$ human-annotated flaws, \PhyReAct{} finds \textbf{228
($75.0\%$)} under \cref{sec:matching}---$191$ whole, $37$ partial---and finds every annotated flaw
on $91$ of the $149$ clips. Two components drive matches: the fallback verifier gpt-5.6-sol overrode
a dense-verifier pass to contradiction on $83$ of the $228$, and the on-screen-text recognizer
transcribes malformed glyphs literally rather than normalizing them, so string comparison catches
garbled text.

\subsubsection{Comparison against published evaluators}\label{sec:external}
We compare \PhyReAct{} against evaluators that localize defects---question-decomposition and scene-graph methods (per-question answers linkable to claims) and free-text evaluators (unstructured flaw lists)---and separately test whether CLIPScore tracks aggregate clip properties. Modular video QA is listed for context but not evaluated here; clip-level agreement with human ratings is deferred to \cref{tab:vs2}.

The same served model, run in a single pass over the prompt and every frame without planning, tools,
or per-claim verification, matches $222$ of the $304$ flaws at one call per clip; \cref{fig:external}
reports it separately because free-text lists are prompt-sensitive and carry no per-verdict
evidence.

Question decomposition matches \textbf{164 of the $304$ flaws ($53.9\%$)}---$125$ whole, $39$
partial---against \PhyReAct{}'s $228$, on the same $149$ densely-read clips through the same model
(identical claim lists on $148$). It asks one closed question per claim and accepts the answer,
whereas \PhyReAct{} plans measurements and may apply several checks per claim. Its higher recall does
not reflect a higher per-finding match rate: $29\%$ of its $557$ findings match a human flaw, against
$26\%$ of \PhyReAct{}'s $871$. Roughly a third of its questions ask only whether an object is present
(\emph{Is there a vending machine?}), answerable from one frame; twelve clips receive no finding.
Clip by clip it finds fewer flaws than \PhyReAct{} on $70$, the same on $63$, more on $16$.

As a clip-level diagnostic, CLIPScore correlates across the $149$ clips with severity at $r=+0.035$
($p=0.67$) and flaw count at $r=-0.024$ ($p=0.77$); with $86$ clips carrying multiple flaws ($241$ of
$304$), a single scalar cannot localize them.

By the annotators' own categories (\cref{tab:headline}), recall runs from $90\%$ to $49\%$. Camera
and style ($90\%$) and on-screen text ($84\%$) rank highest, both measured directly against an

\subsubsection{Specialist measurement traces}
\label{sec:cases}
\Cref{fig:criticcases} presents six matched findings from an earlier physics-routing pass over the evaluation core; they are not from the run of \cref{tab:headline} and do not contribute to its recall. Five rest on numeric measurements and the text case on a recognition-head transcript compared as a string. \paragraph{What each measurement discriminates.} Each catches what an impression would not
(\cref{fig:criticcases}). The \emph{dolphin} leap is contradicted by a near-zero vertical excursion
a motion-only test would have passed; the \emph{bottle}'s surface and background move alike, so its
rolling is camera motion; the \emph{sun} is scored by peak departure (a rise then fall cancels) and
falls short amid comparable camera motion; \emph{counting} compares asserted against measured counts
exactly; and the \emph{text} head returns garbled glyphs that fail string equality, which a
generative model would silently normalize to the requested phrase.

\paragraph{Independent measurements and coverage.} A second dolphin sentence asserts the animal never
disappears, so two specialists evaluated the prompt's requirement that it disappear: the
vertical-travel check required downward motion but measured $2.07$ radii \emph{upward} ($-317$\,px
over $37$ frames, negative denoting up), and the persistence check required it to vanish but found it
on $121$ of $121$ frames with no gap of three or more, so both independently contradict the
requirement. Each measurement also records its temporal coverage: in the banner case the recognition
head read $25$ of the clip's $241$ frames at stride ten, and a transcript stable across those $25$
evidences a persistent banner, whereas a briefly-appearing caption could fall between samples.

\paragraph{Specialist abstention.} In the run of \cref{tab:headline}, physical specialists were
called on $227$ checks, abstaining on $166$ and contradicting on $61$, each abstention carrying its
reason. The most common is no candidate grounding passing the semantic guard---shown one frame with
the candidate boxes and asked only whether they are the named subject: for seagulls scattering
behind fighter jets, the ``seagulls'' boxes held the jets and were discarded, yielding \unknownv{}
and separating a failure to find the subject from a violation by it.

\subsubsection{Agreement with human clip-level ratings}
\label{sec:vs2}
Since localized recall and clip-level scalars are not directly comparable, we compare \PhyReAct{}
against VideoScore2~\cite{videoscore2_2025} on human clip-level semantic-adherence ratings, both on
the same $1$--$5$ scale and scored with VideoScore2's published script. The sample is a prespecified

\subsubsection{Held-out evaluation on Physion-Eval}\label{sec:physion}
Physion-Eval (\cref{sec:bench-vs}) provides clips held out from this system's development: $9{,}569$
generated clips from five generators, each with a human report of its physical glitches ($8{,}745$
with at least one). Two release limits bound what is measurable: the matched real-world reference
videos for the benchmark's $J$ statistic are withheld for copyright, and glitch-free clips are not
verified negatives. The release thus supports neither $J$ nor false-alarm rates for any
evaluator---only flag rates on release clips and matched-report rates on reported-glitch clips.

\PhyReAct{} is being run over a fixed randomized stream; the figures are interim, covering the first
$601$ clips ($543$ with a reported glitch, $58$ without). The in-context lesson state
(\cref{sec:icl-exp}) was active and updated as the stream progressed, so the run is prequential over
an evolving configuration, with no component seeing a label before scoring its clip.

Three measures separate prompt-grounded findings from physics-grounded reports (\cref{tab:physion}).
The \emph{matched-report clip rate} is the share of reported-glitch clips with a finding matching the
report under \cref{sec:matching}. The \emph{evidence-conditioned flag rate} is the share the served
model calls physically unrealistic from the check records alone---question, verdict, reason, and
numerical evidence---without frames or the reported defect. The \emph{anomaly-phrased finding rate}
is the share with a finding that explicitly alleges a physical anomaly rather than an unmet prompt
requirement.

benchmark's published flag rate for every critic it evaluated, with the untrained-human
reference for scale; the full table, adding this work's other two rates and the
benchmark's $J$ statistics, is \cref{tab:physion} in \cref{sec:app-physion}. Because this
work's rows and the benchmark's rows use different samples and system configurations, their
juxtaposition is descriptive rather than a common-protocol comparison.

\subsection{Context-training self-evolution of the critic}
\label{sec:icl-exp}
The critic's inward channel distills lessons from recorded misses into its readable state, improving without touching a weight; the formalism---the distillation map \eqref{eq:distill}, the acceptance gate \eqref{eq:elitism}, and the state $S=(\mathcal{O},M,K)$ it edits---is set out in \cref{sec:framework}. Here we measure the effect on flaw recall.

Applying \eqref{eq:distill} to episodes where a plan missed a human-annotated flaw, $\operatorname{Distill}$ reads the trace and miss and writes a lesson naming a property of the claim and the action it licenses (e.g.\ \emph{when a prompt specifies a singular entity or exact quantity, create a separate cardinality check and verify that count across beginning, middle and end}); a curation pass groups candidates into $33$ \emph{families} of equivalent advice, one lesson each, appended to the planner's rubric. Only $M$ changes ($S' = (\mathcal{O}, M', K)$); a family's name labels which defect its lesson concerns.

The implementation departs from the formalism in two ways. First, all $33$ lessons are supplied on every clip: the selective form (one relevance call per lesson--claim pair) costs $132$ model calls for a four-claim prompt and $495$ for a fifteen-claim one, whereas supplying all costs no calls and adds $9$\,kB to a planner prompt already $17.8$\,kB long. Second, the acceptance gate \eqref{eq:elitism} is dropped: $U_{\mathrm{val}}$ scores only rules already seen in a run, not one from a fresh miss --- as a diagnostic on $80$ earlier candidates over a ten-clip set it admitted none ($39$ failed its minimum-evidence criterion, $41$ never triggered). The evaluated intervention is the curated $33$ lessons, without the gate.

\paragraph{Protocol and result.} Lessons are distilled from experience clips and tested on held-out ones: excluding every clip sharing an identifier or restating a prompt with an experience clip leaves an evaluation set of $306$ clips carrying $502$ flaws, so no lesson is evaluated on the prompt it was written from. Every condition scores those same flaws under the matching protocol of \cref{sec:matching} (a flaw is \emph{found} when the critic covers it wholly or in part), the untaught condition loading an empty lesson file through the same path; five nested conditions---no lessons, then $8$, $13$, $19$ and $33$ lessons distilled from $25$, $50$, $75$ and $117$ experience clips, each pool a prefix of the next---turn the comparison into a dose curve. With all $33$ lessons the planner finds $\mathbf{375}$ of $502$ flaws ($\mathbf{74.7\%}$) against $340$ untaught ($67.7\%$): $62$ flaws switch from missed to found and $27$ the other way, a net $+35$ at $p = 3\!\times\!10^{-4}$ under a paired sign test. More experience does not extend the gain (\cref{fig:iclaggregate}): the four taught conditions find $375$, $377$, $374$ and $375$, each $+34$ to $+37$ over untaught at $p < 10^{-3}$ but no two differing by more than $3$ ($p \geq 0.8$ pairwise), and the unfiltered set ($363$ clips, $626$ flaws) shows the same pattern. The later lessons instead reshuffle which flaws are found without changing how many---$36$ move each way between the $8$- and $33$-lesson conditions---so the gain is already present with lessons from $25$ clips.

\paragraph{Where the lessons point.} The gains are not aligned with the lesson names. All eight families from the first $25$ clips concern actions, none mentioning text, counting or spatial relations, yet at $8$ lessons \emph{on-screen text} posts the largest percentage-point gain of any category ($10$ of $73$) before any lesson mentions text, then loses two after one does; the later $25$ families do not move their nominal targets. \paragraph{Effect of the lessons on the plan.} Lessons compile the prompt into more claims, not more checks per claim: from untaught to $33$ lessons, checks per claim rise only $1.13\!\to\!1.18$ while typed physical claims rise $2{,}905\!\to\!5{,}106$ over the $306$ clips ($9.5\!\to\!16.7$ per clip)---as the decomposition reading predicts. \subsection{Writing a verdict back into generation}\label{sec:refine}
The critic's outward channel is the one claimed but not yet measured: a verdict, with its
evidence, fed back as a prompt rewrite that regenerates the clip. We close that loop on a held-out
generator and ask a deliberately narrow question---does it help on the metric that triggered it, and
does an \emph{independent} rater register the same change?

The loop composes the two maps of \cref{eq:gen-crit-maps} through a rewrite operator. From a base
clip $\hat{\mathcal{V}}\sim\operatorname{Gen}(p,C)$ the critic returns a verdict with its cited
failures,
\begin{equation}
  Y=\operatorname{Crit}(\hat{\mathcal{V}},p)=\bigl(v,\;\pi,\;\mathcal{F}\bigr),
  \qquad v\in\{\textsf{plausible},\textsf{implausible},\textsf{abstain}\},
  \label{eq:refine-verdict}
\end{equation}
$\pi=p_{\text{plausible}}$ the plausibility score and $\mathcal{F}=\{(e_j,a_j)\}_j$ the violated
obligations with their evidence. A text-only rewrite $\rho$ folds those failures back into the
prompt and the \emph{same} generator resamples under the same control and seed,
\begin{equation}
  p' = \rho\bigl(p,\mathcal{F}\bigr)\ \ \text{when}\ \ v=\textsf{implausible}\ \ (\text{else}\ p'=p),
  \qquad
  \hat{\mathcal{V}}' \sim \operatorname{Gen}\bigl(p',C\bigr),
  \label{eq:refine-loop}
\end{equation}
so the channel acts only through the prompt---diffusion weights, control $C$, mask, and seed
untouched---changing appearance while leaving the motion path fixed. We run one round and read the
change in $\pi$ and $v$ against an independent rater applied to both.

\paragraph{Protocol.} We run the full \emph{evaluate\,$\to$\,rewrite\,$\to$\,regenerate}
loop over Cosmos3-Nano, a generator not used anywhere in this system's development, on its
$600$ VideoPhy-2 test clips ($589$ complete the loop and carry a paired score, so the
before/after columns rest on $600$ and $589$). Each baseline and its regeneration are
scored two ways: by \PhyReAct{}'s own metric ($p_{\text{plausible}}$ and the verdict of
\cref{sec:eval}), the quantity the rewrite optimizes; and by VideoPhy-2's official
AutoRater~\cite{videophy2_2025}, an \emph{independent} rater returning semantic adherence
(SA), physical commonsense (PC), and their joint agreement (both $\ge 4$). The AutoRater is
the control: it is not the loop's objective and shares no component with the critic.

\paragraph{On its own metric the loop is effective.} By \PhyReAct{}'s own measurement (\cref{tab:refine-selfmetric}) the loop is targeted with low collateral: it acts on the $14.8\%$ of clips it judges flawed, raises $p_{\text{plausible}}$ on $72.4\%$ of those and flips $49$ of $65$ implausible verdicts to plausible, while regressing only $1.6\%$ of unflagged clips.

\paragraph{An independent rater registers no change.} The same $589$ clips scored by VideoPhy-2's AutoRater show no movement in either dimension (\cref{tab:refine-autorater}; \cref{fig:refinedata} places the two readings side by side, clips in \cref{fig:refinefilm}): joint agreement, SA, and---critically---the \emph{physical} PC dimension all stay flat to lower, within rater noise. State both readings together, not collapsed: the gain appears only on the metric the critic itself assigns, while the one rater independent of the loop registers no change on either axis. The design cannot separate two readings---that rewrites improve a physical quality the critic can measure but the AutoRater cannot, or that they raise the critic's own score without improving the clip---because the critic both triggers and judges the effect it reports. (External position only: Cosmos3-Nano's AutoRater joint score is unchanged by the rewrite, $22.8\!\to\!23.1\%$ (all) and $3.9\!\to\!3.4\%$ (hard), near published human-rated leaderboard entries; but human and AutoRater joint scores share no common protocol, so this is descriptive only.) \paragraph{Scope.} Confirming the rewrites improve physical fidelity as an independent judge would score it needs a rater both independent of the critic and sensitive to the targeted physical property---which the AutoRater's flat PC suggests it is not on these clips. All $589$ per-clip comparisons and both scoring streams ship with the run.

\subsection{Scope and interpretation}\label{sec:eval-scope}
Each setting must be read on its own terms. The generator analyses (\cref{sec:gen-experiments}) are
pre-registered probes on small closed-form scenes, characterizing the deployed pipeline rather than
its generality. Core recall uses recorded annotations on development data, where an unrecorded defect
cannot be scored, and its question-decomposition baseline was specified after inspecting critic
outputs. The context-training result (\cref{sec:icl-exp}) is held out from the distillation pool but
still drawn from the development corpus. Only the interim, prequential Physion-Eval run
(\cref{sec:physion}) is held out from development entirely---with an evolving lesson state, a
different annotation vocabulary, and no frozen-core protocol. The refinement loop
(\cref{sec:refine}) is closed on a held-out generator but confirmed only on the critic's own metric;
the one independent rater shows no change in either dimension.
