\abstract{
Visual fluency in generated video does not imply physical reliability, and a scalar quality
score names neither the obligation a clip violates nor the moment it fails. We present
\PhyReAct{}, an auditable physical-verification system in which a text-only planner compiles
the prompt into typed physical obligations and a statically validated execution plan before
any frame is observed. During execution, observations gate and scope only declared calls to
frozen low-level experts---segmentation and tracking, counting, eleven typed physical
measurements over the resulting tracks, depth, OCR, and audio-event detection. Each action
returns a provenance-carrying evidence record whose payload, when usable, is either a typed
measurement or an explicitly tagged learned state. Typed resolvers and fixed composition map usable records to
a three-valued state (\supported{},
\contradicted{}, or \unknownv{}, surfaced as \emph{plausible},
\emph{implausible}, or \abstain{}) with full provenance, so that every verdict is
traceable to the evidence that produced it. We anchor evaluation in a
1{,}500-clip corpus of human-annotated flaw records that localize real generation
failures in prompt reference, space, and time. On a $149$-clip core carrying $304$
such records, \PhyReAct{} accounts for $228$, against $164$ for a published
question-decomposition evaluator given the same clips and the same claims. Recall alone does
not separate it from prompting the same backbone monolithically, which reaches $222$; what
separates them is that each decision retains its evidence record and provenance, making the
traces auditable one verdict at a time and usable as the interface through which a critic
verdict could be written back into generation. The core is a development set, so these
figures characterize the system rather than its generalization.
}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
% =====================================================================

Contemporary video generation systems can synthesize visually compelling,
temporally coherent clips with synchronized audio. Perceptual plausibility,
however, is not evidence of physical validity: a failure may arise not only in
rendered appearance but in any observable consequence of a physical event.
Within the visual stream, entities may be omitted, trajectories may be
inconsistent with the forces implied by the scene, and interactions may violate
contact or causal constraints. Within the acoustic stream, an expected sound
may be absent, mistimed, or attributed to the wrong source; across modalities,
visual and acoustic observations may encode incompatible event timing or
causality. Thus an object may remain unsupported under gravity, a ball may pass
through a rigid wall, a visible collision may be silent, or its impact sound may
precede contact. These failures become especially consequential as generated
video moves beyond media synthesis into the training and evaluation loops of
embodied systems. Recent video world models have been used to synthesize
robot-training trajectories, support policy learning and evaluation, and reduce
the sim-to-real gap; stronger world models have also been associated with better
downstream policy
performance~\cite{jang2025dreamgen,huang2025enerverse,shang2025roboscape}.
Recently, evaluation has expanded beyond generic video quality to embodied task
correctness and rule-level physical
diagnosis~\cite{li2025worldmodelbench,deng2026rethinking,videophy2_2025,lin2026phyground},
while complementary work has introduced temporal localization and the evaluation
of acoustic or cross-modal physical consistency~\cite{physioneval2026,chinchure2026spotlight,xie2025phyavbench,cui2026avphys}.

Despite this progress, recent evaluators capture complementary components of
physical diagnosis rather than a single auditable evidence chain. They provide
rule-level judgments~\cite{videophy2_2025,lin2026phyground}, temporal
localization~\cite{physioneval2026,chinchure2026spotlight}, measurement-grounded
tests in controlled settings~\cite{wang2026gauge}, and audio-physical or
cross-modal consistency checks~\cite{xie2025phyavbench,zhou2026avgenbench}.
These capabilities are nevertheless typically organized as benchmark-specific
protocols built around curated datasets, predefined phenomena, or manually
authored rubrics. The recent AV-Phys Agent is a particularly close point of
comparison: it combines a reasoning--action loop with deterministic acoustic
tools, but its released protocol remains tied to prompt-specific human-authored
rubrics and multimodal-model binary judgments~\cite{cui2026avphys}. The
ball--wall schematic in \cref{fig:teaser}, which illustrates the interface
rather than a recorded execution, makes the resulting integration gap concrete:
a defensible verdict must establish that the ball and wall are present,
localize floor- and wall-contact windows, measure trajectory and relative
geometry, and determine whether any detected impact sound aligns with the
wall-contact window. Although audio events can be detected in parallel,
synchrony cannot be resolved until that visual window is available. Unusable
evidence leaves the dependent claim unknown; if no claim is contradicted, any
unknown makes the clip abstain, while a terminal infrastructure failure
remains a separate outcome. The gap targeted here is therefore not the absence
of any one component, but their integration within a single evaluator that
compiles the prompt into typed physical obligations and a statically validated
plan, lets observations gate and scope already-declared specialist calls, and
uses fixed rules to compose usable evidence records---typed measurements or
explicitly tagged learned states---into three-valued decisions with traceable
time windows, tool outputs, values, rules, and provenance.

\PhyReAct{} addresses these requirements as a single physical-verification
system coupling a text-only planner, a video-aware semantic verifier, frozen
specialist operators, and fixed composition. For open-ended prompts that provide
no evaluation rubric, the planner operates before any frame is seen, compiling
exact prompt spans into typed physical obligations and a statically validated
execution plan. For evidence distributed across entities, events, time, and
modalities, the verifier reads timestamped frames densely on a shared A/V clock;
its observations activate, skip, or localize only calls already declared by the
plan, and those scoped calls dispatch SAM~3 grounding and
tracking~\cite{sam3_2025}, identity-based counting, eleven track-based physical
measurements, monocular depth, optical character recognition, and audio-event
detection~\cite{flexsed2025}. For heterogeneous or unusable outputs, each action
emits a claim-bound record containing either a typed measurement or an explicitly
tagged learned base state, together with its realized scope,
measured/abstained/errored status, artifact hashes, cost, and provenance. Typed
resolvers and fixed composition map usable records to supported, contradicted,
or unknown claim states and then to plausible, implausible, or abstain at clip
level; unavailable evidence remains unknown, while terminal infrastructure
failures remain separate. For opaque verdicts, the system returns an auditable
claim-level trace linking each decision to its prompt span, planned calls,
localized evidence, and composition rule. Together, these components turn an
open prompt and generated A/V clip into a dependency-aware evidence chain rather
than an unordered set of model answers. \Cref{fig:teaser} contrasts this design
with the evaluated question-decomposition baseline, which asks one closed
yes/no question per claim without a measurement trace.

To evaluate the critic at the granularity of its trace, we assemble a corpus of
$1{,}500$ generated clips with $2{,}582$ human-written, prompt-grounded flaw
records. Each record quotes the violated prompt span and provides a rationale,
severity, confidence, and, when available, a temporal span and object track;
these records are the only ground truth used here. A flaw-level protocol scores
each system finding as whole, partial, or missed against the corresponding
human record. On a $149$-clip recall analysis, \PhyReAct{} accounts for $228$ of
$304$ annotated defects, compared with $164$ for a published
question-decomposition evaluator given the same clips, claims, and served model.
A monolithic prompt to that same backbone accounts for $222$, so recall alone
does not isolate the value of the agentic organization; the principal
distinction is that every \PhyReAct{} decision retains its supporting evidence
and provenance. This analysis is recall-only, single-annotator, and not held
out, and therefore characterizes the current system rather than its
generalization (\cref{sec:eval}).

In summary, our contributions are threefold: (i) \PhyReAct{}, an integrated
physical-verification system that compiles prompts into typed obligations,
scopes specialist operators with observations, and returns three-valued
decisions with localized evidence and provenance; (ii) the $1{,}500$-clip corpus
and its flaw-level matching protocol; and (iii) a simulation-conditioned
generation testbed in which known MuJoCo geometry is rendered as depth or
silhouette control for a frozen Wan~2.2-VACE generator
~\cite{todorov2012mujoco,wan2025,vace2025}. The current report evaluates the
critic and generator separately. Together, these contributions provide an
auditable evaluation stack and a concrete interface for future physical
refinement.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{Physics-aware generation and refinement.}
Controllable video generation separates appearance synthesis from structural
guidance by conditioning diffusion models on depth, segmentation, reference
frames, masks, motion fields, and compositional spatiotemporal signals
~\cite{zhang2023controlnet,wang2023videocomposer,wan2025,vace2025}. Such
interfaces determine how geometric information enters a generator, but do not
by themselves establish that either the condition or the synthesized pixels
obey physical laws. A complementary line derives control from physical
reasoning or simulation: scene properties can be inferred and reconstructed for
simulation, simulated trajectories or flow can guide synthesis, textual
physical context can refine a prompt, and language can be compiled into a
coarse motion plan. More recent research introduces continuous controls over
physical properties or couples simulation and rendering directly with generation
~\cite{motioncraft2024,physgen2024,diffphy2025,phyt2v2025,vlipp2025,
phyco2026,psivg2026,moregen2026}. Evaluation feedback has also been used for
preference optimization, verifier-guided candidate search, and localized
regeneration~\cite{aifeedback2024,videot1_2025,videorepair2026}. These studies
pursue both pre-synthesis physical conditioning and evaluation-guided
refinement. The present work decouples these two stages: simulator-derived
metric depth or silhouette controls condition a frozen video generator, while
\PhyReAct{} independently evaluates the resulting clip. This design distinguishes
adherence to the control signal from physical validity in the synthesized
output; feeding diagnoses back into regeneration remains future work.

\paragraph{Model judgments, decomposition, and physical evaluation.}
Automated video evaluation has progressed from aggregate metrics toward
increasingly structured model judgments. General protocols factor perceptual
quality, temporal consistency, and prompt alignment, while learned VLM or MLLM
judges map a prompt and clip to multidimensional ratings, discrete states, or
natural-language explanations
~\cite{vbench2024,t2vcompbench2024,fetv2023,evalcrafter2024,
videoscore2024,videoscore2_2025}. Prompt-decomposition methods instead translate
a prompt into propositions, questions, or query chains and revisit the visual
input through smaller checks~\cite{tifa2023,dsg2024,vqascore2024,
han2025videobench}; the checks are explicit, although their supporting evidence
is generally still produced by the judging model. Physics-oriented benchmarks
further introduce
phenomenon-, rule-, trajectory-, and prompt-specific criteria, criterion-level
reasoning, failure localization, controlled measurements, and audiovisual
consistency~\cite{videophy2024,videophy2_2025,phygenbench2024,physicsiq2025,
phycobench2025,phyworldbench2026,lin2026phyground,physioneval2026,
chinchure2026spotlight,luo2026lovif,wang2026gauge,xie2025phyavbench,
zhou2026avgenbench,cui2026avphys}. Prior physical evaluation is therefore not
uniformly scalar, unlocalized, visual-only, or unmeasured; rather, these
capabilities remain distributed across evaluators with different scopes, output
contracts, and evidence representations.

\paragraph{Execution harnesses, context engineering, and persistent state.}
Execution harnesses separate orchestration from model judgment by making
planning, dependencies, specialist routing, observation scope, validation,
termination, and trace logging explicit
~\cite{toolformer2023,gpt4tools2023,hugginggpt2023,rewoo2023,llmcompiler2024}.
Modular visual and video methods extend this organization with specialized
perception, selective observation, and adaptive temporal sampling
~\cite{visprog2023,vipergpt2023,codevqa2023,proviq2023,morevqa2024,
videoagent_wang2024,videoagent_fan2024,videotree2025,caviar2025,lenswalk2026},
while video-evaluation pipelines already combine prompt structuring with
temporal tools, formal specifications and model checking, localized repair,
deterministic acoustic measurements, or prompt-specific rubrics
~\cite{videogeneval2025,neusv2025,neuse2025,cui2026avphys}. Across episodes, a
harness also constructs the context for each model call by storing, retrieving,
filtering, and formatting persistent state; prior work distills trajectories
into reflective lessons, reusable strategies, or structured knowledge without
weight updates, and separates recorded evidence from derived beliefs to reduce
context pollution~\cite{explicitmem2026,contexttraining2026,reasoningbank2026,
reflexion2023,plugmem2026,hindsight2026}. These components predate our work, and
controlled studies show that additional tools or scaffolding may increase cost
without improving accuracy~\cite{findeis2025external,kapoor2026hal}. Relative
to this literature, \PhyReAct{} contributes their integration under a common
verification contract: open prompts are compiled into typed obligations and a
validated plan, observations only gate or scope declared calls, measurements
remain distinct from learned states, and fixed rules produce initial
three-valued claims with any review override preserved separately in the trace.
Across episodes, it uses offline lessons from a disjoint experience pool and
separately evaluates an evolving in-context lesson state
(\cref{sec:selfevolve:measured,sec:physion}). The reported results therefore
establish experience-conditioned harness execution, not autonomous
self-improvement or open-ended replanning; automatic changes to tools,
workflows, admission policies, and external knowledge remain future work.

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}
% =====================================================================

This section details our three components: a physics-guided video generator (\cref{sec:generation}), a human-annotated video-flaw benchmark (\cref{sec:dataset}), and \PhyReAct{}, an auditable multi-tool critic (\cref{sec:critic}).

\subsection{Physics-Guided Generation}\label{sec:generation}

Our generation pipeline separates a solver-defined motion target from the appearance request.
Given a physical event, it authors and validates a structured scene, simulates the resulting
trajectory, and renders registered geometric controls. A control-conditioned diffusion backbone
then receives the control video together with an appearance prompt, a generation mask, and a
conditioning strength (\cref{fig:genpipe}). The control therefore encodes a
solver-consistent target; whether the generated video follows that target remains an empirical
question evaluated in \cref{sec:gen-experiments}.

The implementation instantiates this interface in two ways. Simple prompt-authored events use a
planar simulator and silhouette controls, whereas the curated three-dimensional scene library
uses MuJoCo and depth controls. Both routes expose the same three stages described below:
scene authoring and validation, registered simulation-to-control rendering, and
control-conditioned video synthesis.

\subsubsection{Physics specification, simulation, and validation}\label{sec:gen-authoring}

The authoring stage maps a description of the required event to a structured scene
specification containing bodies, initial states, colliders, static surfaces, a camera, and
event-specific validation predicates (\cref{fig:authoring}).

Each body is either simulated from its initial state or assigned prescribed waypoints. The first
form is used when the engine should determine the motion, such as a ballistic arc and its
contacts. Waypoints are reserved for externally actuated or explicitly counterfactual motion
that is not determined by the simulated forces.

Validation is applied to the simulated trajectory rather than to the specification text. For
example, a ballistic segment must form an arc, satisfy the required hit or miss relation, settle
when requested, and remain visible. A failed predicate is returned to the authoring model with a
summary of the realized motion, and the specification is revised for at most three rounds. A
passing trajectory is marked \emph{validated}; exhausting the retry budget yields an explicitly
marked unvalidated fallback rather than a validated scene.

The planar route renders validated trajectories as silhouettes. The three-dimensional route
uses rigid bodies directly and represents cloth, ropes, chains, and granular scenes with
particle-and-link primitives; buoyancy is applied as an external force on rigid bodies. In both
routes the control is rendered from solver state, not inferred from a generated video.

\subsubsection{Registered simulation-to-control rendering}\label{sec:gen-control}

At each output time, the renderer queries the shaded view, depth buffer, and object-segmentation
buffer from the same solver state. The resulting streams are therefore registered pixel for
pixel and share a common frame clock. The three-dimensional route uses MuJoCo~\cite{todorov2012mujoco};
the planar route exposes the same state--render interface. Solver, camera, and playback settings
used in the experiments are reported in \cref{sec:gen-setup}. The camera declared in the
specification is applied to the validated scene before rendering: a pan becomes a slow uniform
drift of the whole scene, an orbit or push-in is left to the generator, and bodies that serve only
as on-screen graphics are dropped at this step. A contact sheet of the rendered clip is saved
alongside the control video.

The depth buffer is converted to a near-bright, far-dark control using robust clip-level
percentiles. For depth frame $D_t$ over pixel domain $\Omega$ and
$t\in\{0,\ldots,T-1\}$, let
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{D}
  &= \{D_t(u):t\in\{0,\ldots,T-1\},\ u\in\Omega,\ D_t(u)\neq d_{\mathrm{far}}\},\\
d_- &= Q_{0.01}(\mathcal{D}),\qquad
d_+ = Q_{0.97}(\mathcal{D}),\\
C_t(u)
  &= 1-\operatorname{clip}\!\left(
       \frac{D_t(u)-d_-}{d_+-d_-},\,0,\,1\right).
\end{split}
\label{eq:global-depth-control}
\end{equation}
Here $d_{\mathrm{far}}$ is the background constant and the quantiles are computed over all
frames, so the same distance maps to the same intensity throughout the clip. A small symmetric
Gaussian blur softens rasterized boundaries without shifting their half-intensity contour. If
$d_+=d_-$, or if a predeclared flat-scene template provides insufficient depth contrast, the
segmentation stream is rendered instead as a binary moving-body silhouette. Depth is otherwise
preferred because it preserves both outline and distance ordering.

\subsubsection{Physics-conditioned video synthesis}\label{sec:gen-mechanism}

The generator receives four inputs: an appearance prompt $p$, a geometric control video $C$, a
generation mask $M$, and a conditioning strength $s$. We instantiate the interface with a
control-conditioned video-diffusion backbone~\cite{zhang2023controlnet,wang2023videocomposer,vace2025}.
The prompt describes the subject, material, and setting, while $C$ encodes the target geometry
and motion rendered by \cref{sec:gen-control}.

The mask specifies which pixels may be synthesized. We use an all-white mask, so every output
pixel is generated and no pixel from the simulator render is copied into the clip. The control
therefore acts only through the backbone's conditioning stream. Writing $h_i^{(\ell)}$ for the
hidden state at denoising step $i$ and layer $\ell$, $z^{\mathrm{ctrl}}$ for the encoded control,
and $W^{(\ell)}$ for the corresponding projection, the deployed update is
\begin{equation}
h_i^{(\ell)} \;\leftarrow\; h_i^{(\ell)} \;+\; s\,W^{(\ell)}z^{\mathrm{ctrl}}.
\label{eq:control-release}
\end{equation}
The same scalar $s$ is applied to every control layer, and the control remains active throughout
sampling in the deployed generator. Alongside $p$, $C$, $M$ and $s$, the generator receives a
fixed seed and a set of excluded terms; $s$ is lowered when the control covers little of the frame.
The release-time interventions in \cref{sec:gen-release} are mechanism probes and are not part of
this deployment rule.

\subsection{A Human-Annotated Video-Flaw Benchmark}\label{sec:dataset}
The benchmark is a corpus of $1{,}500$ AI-generated clips in which a person watched each
clip against its prompt and wrote down every place the video failed to deliver what the
text asked for. Every clip carries at least one such record. The generating model is not
recorded in the data, and no clip is attributed to a specific system. The human-written
fields are the ground truth throughout this report.

\subsubsection{What a flaw record contains}\label{sec:bench-spec}
A flaw is a localized record with six fields. Four are always present: the
\textbf{violated fragment}, the words of the prompt the video fails, quoted verbatim; a
free-text \textbf{rationale}; a \textbf{severity}; and the annotator's
\textbf{confidence}. Two are present when the annotator could supply them: a
\textbf{time span}, the first and last frame in which the failure is visible, and a
\textbf{box track}, boxes at the start, middle and end of that span, one per object
involved, in normalized coordinates. \Cref{tab:flawfields} gives each field's measured
coverage across the evaluation core, and \cref{fig:benchanatomy} shows one record in
full. Each clip additionally carries an overall severity and a short list of the objects,
actions and relations its prompt calls for.

Two coarse tags support grouping and reporting: a \emph{modality} tag saying whether the
complaint concerns what is seen, what is heard, or both, and a \emph{type} tag naming the
dominant kind of complaint from a fixed ten-item list. Neither is written by the
annotator. Both are assigned by three language models classifying each flaw from its
quoted fragment and rationale at temperature zero under a fixed seed, resolved by
majority; a three-way disagreement resolves to ``both'' for modality and ``other'' for
type, and a flaw receiving no usable vote falls back to a frozen keyword rule. They are
labelled machine-assigned wherever they appear and are never treated as ground truth.
Annotation is single-annotator: the corpus carries one annotation per flaw, so no
inter-annotator agreement figure is available.

\subsubsection{How the evaluation set is derived}
Three recorded stages narrow $1{,}500$ clips to the $149$ every recall figure in this report
is measured over. \Cref{tab:datastats} gives the counts and \cref{fig:benchcomp} the same
derivation as a sequence of steps.

\emph{Stage one, clip selection.} A clip enters the pool if its video is retrievable and
it shows signs of dynamics. The dynamics test is a fixed vocabulary of motion
words---falling, bouncing, rolling, colliding, pouring, toppling---applied to the prompt
and, separately, to each flaw's rationale and quoted fragment; a clip is kept when its
prompt contains one, or one of its flaws does, or it has a time-windowed flaw against an
action-typed object. The test is a keyword rule and selects for prompts and complaints
that \emph{talk about} motion, which is a proxy for, not a guarantee of, a flaw whose
adjudication needs physical reasoning. This stage keeps $606$ of the $1{,}500$ clips,
carrying $1{,}171$ flaws.

\emph{Stage two, flaw selection.} Within those clips, a flaw is a usable detection target
only if a critic seeing only pixels could in principle find it. Flaws tagged ``seen'' or
``both'' are kept and purely audible complaints dropped, which removes $64$ flaws and
leaves $1{,}107$ as the full evaluation set.

\emph{Stage three, the frozen core.} A core of $150$ clips with $306$ flaws is drawn for
routine measurement. The draw is deterministic and its contract recorded: a fixed seed;
strata crossing overall clip severity, flaw count per clip and clip duration;
largest-remainder proportional allocation across strata; within each stratum, candidates
ordered lexicographically then shuffled once by a generator whose call sequence is fixed;
and a replacement rule drawing from the same stratum for any clip that cannot be
downloaded or decoded. A continuity-focused stratum of $29$ clips and $85$ flaws sits
inside the core and is exempt from replacement. Every clip is downloaded and decoded
before the core is written, and the selection, strata and replacements are stored with
the set.

The core is an \emph{optimization set}: system and prompt decisions are made against it,
so results on it measure fit to that core, and a generalization claim requires a set that
has not influenced system design. A separate pool of $711$ annotated clips is held
disjoint from both frozen sets for any learning that consumes this data.

One clip of the $150$ produced no measurement in the evaluated run and is absent from every
figure in \cref{sec:eval}: a ten-second classroom scene carrying two flaws, both marked at the
highest severity. Recall is therefore reported over $149$ clips and the $304$ flaw entries they
carry, and never over the full $306$. Those two flaws are excluded rather than counted as missed,
because nothing was measured on that clip either way, which makes the reported rate very slightly
optimistic.

\subsubsection{What is in the evaluation core}
The core is $150$ clips, median $8.0$\,s long (range $5.0$--$10.3$\,s), mostly at
$24$\,fps ($118$ of $150$; the rest at 16, 25, 30 or 32). The prompts are not one-line
captions: a median prompt is $42$ words and the longest $188$, and many specify a shot
list, a count, an ordering, an on-screen string and a sound in one paragraph. Sixty-six
of the $150$ clips carry flaws of more than one kind, so a single number per clip cannot
say which of them a detector found.

Its $306$ flaws, a median of two per clip, group by machine-assigned type as: actions
that do not happen or happen wrongly, $139$; wrong counts, $36$; wrong or missing
objects, $30$; garbled on-screen text, $25$; wrong spatial arrangement, $19$; wrong
ordering in time, $17$; wrong attributes such as colour or material, $16$; camera and
style, $10$; physical motion, $7$; and a residual $7$. The largest category is whether
the requested action occurred at all, so this is not a physics benchmark in the narrow
sense. Counting, text and spatial arrangement together account for $80$ flaws, each a
question a general-purpose model answers poorly and a dedicated measurement answers well.

Of the $237$ flaws carrying a time span, the flagged window covers a median of $62\%$ of
the clip, and the distribution is bimodal: $57$ are confined to a quarter of the clip or
less, and $95$ run for at least nine-tenths of it (\cref{fig:benchdist}). A detector
needs both a way to look closely at a moment and a way to judge a whole clip.

\subsubsection{Scoring conventions}
Every clip carries at least one flaw. With no clean clips, recall is directly measurable
and precision is not: a detector that flags everything scores perfectly on recall alone,
so results on this set are reported alongside the rate at which accusations land on a
real flaw.

Two counts describe the same core, and they differ. Eleven clips record the same failure
two or three times under different identifiers, seventeen duplicate entries in all, so the
core's $306$ entries are $289$ distinct flaws. Recall in \cref{sec:eval} is reported over
entries rather than distinct spans, because the matching protocol there assigns each finding
to at most one entry and a duplicate entry can be claimed only once; the $149$ clips it
scores carry $304$ of the $306$.

\subsubsection{Relation to existing video benchmarks}\label{sec:bench-vs}
The closest annotated resource is Physion-Eval~\cite{physioneval2026}, which pairs
generated clips with real-world reference processes and provides expert reasoning traces
containing temporally localized glitches, a physical-failure category, and an
explanation. VideoPhy-2~\cite{videophy2_2025} instead has three annotators rate semantic
adherence and physical commonsense, and mark predefined physical rules as violated,
followed, or undetermined. Per-dimension suites such as VBench~\cite{vbench2024} and
T2V-CompBench~\cite{t2vcompbench2024} primarily support comparison among generators on
fixed prompt collections.

Our corpus targets a complementary, prompt-grounded detector protocol. Each record
attaches a failure to the exact words of the generation prompt and permits a time span
and box track, so an allegation can be matched to a particular annotated failure rather
than only correlated with a clip-level score. Unlike Physion-Eval, this protocol does
not require a matched real-world reference video; unlike VideoPhy-2, it uses
open-vocabulary flaw statements rather than a fixed candidate-rule list. These choices
make claim-level miss attribution possible in \cref{sec:eval}, but they do not improve
annotation reliability: the present corpus is single-annotator and contains only flawed
clips, whereas VideoPhy-2 provides redundant judgments and majority resolution.

\subsection{\PhyReAct{}: Auditable Physical Verification}\label{sec:critic}
\label{sec:compiler}

\subsubsection{\PhyReAct{} as a physically grounded reasoning--action system}
\label{sec:critic-react}

\PhyReAct{} uses a constrained reasoning--action--observation structure related to
prior tool-using agents~\cite{react2023}. A text-only reasoner interleaves
\emph{reason}, \emph{act}, and \emph{observe} steps, and the result of each action
conditions what the agent does next. The
reasoning step compiles the evaluation prompt into a set of \emph{typed physical
claims} together with an executable measurement plan---the physical obligations
that the video must satisfy for the prompt to hold. Each \emph{act} step
dispatches a frozen low-level physical operator (grounding and
tracking with SAM~3~\cite{sam3_2025}, counting over its instance identities, one of
eleven typed physical measurements over those tracks, depth, optical character
recognition, or audio event grounding with FlexSED~\cite{flexsed2025}) or a
dense-frame semantic verifier over the sampled frames. The \emph{observe} step
consumes the returned measurement and uses it to gate whether a claimed event
occurred, to scope which frames and entities subsequent operators attend to,
and---via fixed, deterministic rules---to compute the physical relations that the
typed plan declared. Tool outputs therefore steer execution, within obligations declared
before any pixel was read.

Open-ended reasoning is placed entirely at the front. The planner compiles and
\emph{statically validates} the typed measurement plan before observing the video, following
plan-before-act agents~\cite{rewoo2023,llmcompiler2024} and code-as-plan systems that emit an
executable program of visual primitives~\cite{vipergpt2023,visprog2023}. At execution time
observations activate, skip, or localize nodes the plan already declares. Because the
trajectory is fixed before execution, every measurement traces to the obligation that
requested it.

Specialist operators return typed measurements, while learned semantic base states are
explicitly tagged in their evidence records. Deterministic composition over usable records
produces the verdict. This division---evidence producers record, fixed rules adjudicate---is
what yields a traceable chain of evidence behind every
\supported{}/\contradicted{}/\unknownv{} decision.

\paragraph{Compiled closures as reusable operators.}
The compiled closure of a typed check --- its input scoping, the operator invocations it
triggers, and the deterministic rule turning their measurements into a relation --- is
self-contained. No part of a closure is specific to one video, and each already carries the
per-node cost accounting recorded in its provenance trace, so closures can in principle be
named, versioned, and retrieved across tasks in the manner of procedural-memory
proposals~\cite{plugmem2026,reasoningbank2026}. \PhyReAct{} as implemented evaluates every
clip independently and retains nothing between clips; curating the closures into such a
library is future work (\cref{sec:selfevolve}).

\subsubsection{Problem formulation and system overview}
A generated clip is paired with the prompt $p$ that requested it. We represent
the decoded video by its own container clock,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{V}
  = \{(I_t,\tau_t)\}_{t\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal V}},
\qquad
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal V}=\{0,\ldots,T_{\mathcal V}-1\},
\qquad
0\leq\tau_0<\cdots<\tau_{T_{\mathcal V}-1}\leq D_{\mathcal V},
\label{eq:video-clock}
\end{equation}
where $I_t$ is frame $t$, $\tau_t$ is its timestamp, and $D_{\mathcal V}$ is
the clip duration. The explicit range on $t$ separates a frame index from time
in seconds and also permits nonuniform decode timestamps. An audio track, when
present, is addressed on the same clock.

\PhyReAct{} has four roles. A \emph{text-only planner} decomposes the full prompt
and writes a verification program before seeing any frame. A \emph{video-aware
semantic verifier} reads timestamped frames densely for predicates such as
existence and event occurrence. \emph{Specialist instruments} return targeted
measurements such as counts, tracks, masks, depths, text, and sound intervals.
Finally, a \emph{deterministic aggregator} applies typed predicates and
three-valued composition. The planner and semantic verifier may use the same
served checkpoint, but they are separate calls with different inputs and
responsibilities.

The planner produces exact prompt-grounded claims and a surface plan,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}(p)
  = \{c_i=(s_i,\phi_i)\}_{i=1}^{N_p},
\qquad s_i\sqsubseteq p,
\qquad
(\mathcal{C},\widetilde{\Pi})=\Pi_{\theta}(p),
\label{eq:claims-and-plan}
\end{equation}
where $s_i$ is a verbatim span of $p$ and $\phi_i$ is the checkable assertion
attached to it. The relation $s_i\sqsubseteq p$ is enforced, not inferred after
execution. Types belong to the plan's operations and checks rather than to an
unverifiable free-form claim label.

\Cref{fig:criticpipe} summarizes the resulting dataflow. Planning is
\emph{global with respect to the prompt}: the naming pass sees the whole prompt
and all later claim groups share that vocabulary. Execution can be
\emph{local in time}: a declared interval may gate or scope a later declared
action. This is the plan--execute branch of the reason--act--observe family
(\cref{sec:critic-react}): observations activate, skip, or localize nodes already
in the program, but they do not search over alternative plans or invent a new
tool recipe during execution.

\subsubsection{Prompt claims and text-only plan synthesis}
\label{sec:plan-construction}
Claim extraction preserves both the exact source span $s_i$ and its normalized
assertion $\phi_i$. A first pass over the whole prompt assigns stable identifiers
to every distinct entity and to every separately asserted event occurrence.
The claims are then planned in groups of at most four. Each group sees the full
prompt, the shared names, and only its assigned claims; its local identifiers
are renamed mechanically before all groups are assembled. This construction
prevents two groups from silently referring to the same entity by different
names or to different occurrences by one name.

The surface language follows code-as-plan visual programming
~\cite{vipergpt2023,visprog2023}, but is deliberately restricted to the
operations in \cref{tab:planops}. For claim $c_i$, the planner writes
\code{check(ci, <expr>)} with one operation or a conjunction of operations. At
this stage the planner has no video input: the line states what evidence would
settle the claim, not whether the claim is true.

\paragraph{Bounded recovery.}
The assembled surface plan is validated as a whole because its important
constraints are cross-references: every check must consume existing actions,
every identifier must denote a shared entity or event, and every claim must
have a check. A failing group may be asked again with its own parser or type
complaints and a restatement of the format, but never with a suggested answer.
After a fixed number of attempts, an unusable claim falls back to direct
semantic verification. The fallback is recorded and charged as a model call;
it is not counted as a specialist measurement. A failed naming pass sends all
claims through the same charged fallback, so plan failure never makes a prompt
appear cheaper by silently dropping claims.

\subsubsection{Typed plan representation and static validation}
\label{sec:plan-lang}
The compiler turns the surface program into a typed directed graph
\begin{equation}
G_{\Pi}=(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{Q},\mathcal{E},b),
\qquad
a_j=(o_j,\alpha_j,\omega_j,r_j),
\qquad
b:\mathcal{Q}\rightarrow\{1,\ldots,N_p\}.
\label{eq:plan-dag}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal{A}$ contains evidence-producing actions, including general
semantic passes and specialist calls; $\mathcal{Q}$ contains typed checks;
$\mathcal{E}$ points from a prerequisite to its consumer; and $b(q)=i$ binds
check $q$ to claim $c_i$. An action records its operation $o_j$, arguments
$\alpha_j$, declared scope policy $\omega_j$, and result type $r_j$.

Before any action runs, the graph must satisfy
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Valid}(G_{\Pi})
=
\operatorname{Parse}(G_{\Pi})
\land \operatorname{Refs}(G_{\Pi})
\land \operatorname{Typed}(G_{\Pi})
\land \operatorname{Acyclic}(G_{\Pi})
\land
\left[\forall i,\ \exists q\in\mathcal{Q}:b(q)=i\right].
\label{eq:plan-validity}
\end{equation}
$\operatorname{Refs}$ checks identifiers and dependency closure;
$\operatorname{Typed}$ checks operator arity, fixed relation vocabularies, and
whether every check receives the result types it requires. For example, a
directed trajectory must carry a target, and a depth-ordering check must consume
both masks and depth. An invalid whole line falls back to the general verifier;
an invalid term can be replaced by a recorded generalist sub-check. In both
cases the substitution is charged and reported rather than guessed.
\Cref{fig:plangraph} shows a compiled plan of this form, with the dependency
closure that \cref{eq:plan-validity} requires made explicit.

\subsubsection{Timeline-conditioned multi-tool execution}
\label{sec:chaining}
Execution follows a topological order. Each action's compiled scope function
$g_j$ converts prerequisite records into either the full frame index set or a
declared local subset, after which the appropriate backend is called:
\begin{equation}
\Omega_j
  =g_j\!\left(\{R_k:(a_k,a_j)\in\mathcal{E}\},
              \mathcal{T}_{\mathcal V}\right),
\qquad
R_j
  =F_{o_j}\!\left(\mathcal{V}|_{\Omega_j},\alpha_j\right).
\label{eq:scoped-execution}
\end{equation}
The scope function is part of the validated program. A usable measured window is
padded, given a minimum duration, and converted to the explicit indices whose
timestamps fall inside it. A missing, invalid, or overly broad window falls
back to the whole clip. This conservative fallback avoids interpreting failure
to localize as evidence that the requested event or relation is absent.

\paragraph{Occurrence gates timing.}
A returned time window is not evidence that the queried event occurred: a
forced localizer may return its best candidate even for an absent event.
Visible events are therefore confirmed first by the dense-frame semantic
verifier, while audible events are confirmed on the audio track. Only supported
occurrences allow their intervals to enter a temporal predicate. For a relation
$R\in\{\mathrm{before},\mathrm{during}\}$,
\begin{equation}
z_{R(A,B)}
=
\begin{cases}
\mathsf{C}, & \mathsf{C}\in\{o_A,o_B\},\\
\mathsf{U}, & \mathsf{U}\in\{o_A,o_B\}
              \text{ or }W_A\text{ or }W_B\text{ is unavailable},\\
\rho_R(W_A,W_B), & o_A=o_B=\mathsf{S},
\end{cases}
\label{eq:temporal-gate}
\end{equation}
where $o_A,o_B$ are occurrence states and $W_A,W_B$ are measured windows.
Thus timing may compose an order but can never establish occurrence.

\paragraph{Temporal arithmetic.}
For positive-duration windows $W_X=[s_X,e_X]$, the recorded executor uses a
boundary slack equal to one quarter of the shorter window:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\delta(A,B)=\tfrac14
  \min(e_A-s_A,e_B-s_B),\\
\rho_{\mathrm{before}}(W_A,W_B)
=
\begin{cases}
\mathsf{S}, & e_A\leq s_B+\delta(A,B),\\
\mathsf{C}, & e_B\leq s_A+\delta(A,B),\\
\mathsf{U}, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:temporal-before}
\end{equation}
The slack absorbs small endpoint uncertainty, so it can tolerate a boundary
overlap no larger than $\delta$; if neither direction passes, the intervals do
not resolve an order and the state is $\mathsf{U}$. With the quarter-window rule
and positive durations, the first two cases cannot both hold. Containment is
directional and uses the same boundary slack: clear containment supports, clear
reverse containment contradicts, and boundary-indistinguishable cases remain
unknown. These predicates are a tolerance-scaled subset of Allen's interval
algebra~\cite{allen1983intervals}. \Cref{fig:planchain} follows one such
ordering through a recorded run, from the plan line written before any frame was
read to the two measured windows that settle it.

\paragraph{Exact-request reuse.}
Two actions share one execution only when their complete realized signatures
match:
\begin{equation}
\chi_{\mathcal V}(a_j)
  =(\operatorname{id}(\mathcal V),o_j,\alpha_j,r_j,\Omega_j),
\qquad
a_j\sim a_k
\Longleftrightarrow
\chi_{\mathcal V}(a_j)=\chi_{\mathcal V}(a_k).
\label{eq:exact-reuse}
\end{equation}
The executor calls each equivalence class once, following exact
common-subexpression reuse~\cite{sellis1988mqo,roy2000mqo}. Because the realized
scope $\Omega_j$ is part of the signature, whole-clip and localized requests
never merge merely because their natural-language queries are similar.

\subsubsection{The physical measurement operations}
\label{sec:physics}
Physical claims are settled by eleven operations sharing a single grounding step. SAM~3
segments the named subject and carries a persistent identity for it across frames, and every
operation measures over those tracks. Each operation corresponds to a class of human complaint
in the corpus.

The largest class is displacement, because the dominant physical complaint is one sentence in many
costumes: the sun remains stationary instead of rising, the tornado stays in place, the plate never
rotates, the clouds are static. Something the prompt sets in motion does not move, or moves the
wrong way. Complaints resembling a ballistic arc are rare by comparison. Three
conventions make that measurable. Displacement is reported in multiples of the subject's own
radius, so no scene calibration is required and a sun crossing a tenth of its own width is
distinguishable from one crossing three. Direction is taken from the furthest excursion off the
starting position rather than the net, because a rise followed by a fall cancels to nothing.
And camera motion is cancelled by dense optical flow over the whole frame before any subject
motion is reported, which requires no stationary landmark and so remains valid when the camera
and the subject move together.

Three operations address failures that involve no net travel. Contact measures the closest
approach between two tracked masks, which decides whether two things touch at a finer
resolution than box overlap. Shape change measures the subject's own surface motion relative
to the background flow, catching a scroll that rolls when it should lie still --- a case
displacement reports as zero. Path shape reads the trajectory's form, distinguishing a circle
from a figure of eight.

The rest address time and existence. Permanence asks whether a subject that should vanish instead
persists, and hop counting whether one that should leave the ground ever does. Damping detects a
spinning object that never slows, the complaint about the arrow of time. Onset ordering decides
which of two events comes first, and covers the more common case in which one of them never happens
at all. Clip timing handles duration and playback rate, and viewpoint motion separates a moving
camera from a moving subject when the claim concerns the camera.

All eleven return a number with the evidence behind it --- the frames read, the tracker used,
the threshold applied --- and leave the decision to the fixed rules of \cref{sec:semantics}.
All eleven may also abstain: a subject smaller than one percent of the frame, or a track too
short to measure, yields an explicit abstention, so an unmeasurable claim does not resolve as a
supported one.

\subsubsection{Evidence records and provenance}
\label{sec:contract}
Each action is serialized as a claim-bound record
\begin{equation}
R_{j,i}
=
\bigl(\operatorname{id}(\mathcal V),p,s_i,o_j,\Omega_j,
       \sigma_j,\mu_j,h_j,\kappa_j\bigr),
\qquad
\sigma_j\in
\{\mathtt{measured},\mathtt{abstained},\mathtt{errored}\}.
\label{eq:evidence-record}
\end{equation}
The payload $\mu_j$ contains either a typed measurement or an explicitly tagged
learned base state; $h_j$ contains the native-output and artifact hashes; and
$\kappa_j$ contains calls and accelerator time, with optional wall time, price,
and token counts. Shared execution may bind the same record to several claims,
but its cost is charged once by execution identifier.

The three statuses are mutually exclusive. A \code{measured} record carries a
payload and no abstention or error; \code{abstained} carries a reason and no
payload; \code{errored} carries an infrastructure message. A valid negative
measurement---for example a complete usable track
that does not exhibit the requested motion---remains \code{measured} and may
contradict a claim. Failure to track because of blur, occlusion, low quality, or
an instrument refusal is \code{abstained}, not a negative observation.

The counting path violates that boundary. Its contract reduces a per-frame series to a single
number by taking the mode, so a phrase the segmenter grounds intermittently yields a series
such as $[4,6,0,6,6,0,0,0,\ldots]$ whose mode is $0$, and a grounding that failed on most
frames is reported as a measured count of zero. On a clip containing five steel balls the
operation returns exactly that: $0$ over $241$ frames read, accompanied by \code{stable: false}
and a range of $0$ to $8$. A consumer reading the scalar alone therefore receives a confident
wrong number, and the auxiliary fields are load-bearing. In the present payload a zero meaning
\emph{absent} is indistinguishable from a zero meaning \emph{never found};
\cref{sec:limitations} gives the consequence for count-derived figures.
A terminal \code{errored} record is retried and, if still terminal, produces
no semantic verdict; the clip is marked $\bot_{\mathrm{infra}}$ and excluded
from the completed evaluation set rather than relabeled \unknownv{}.

Specialist schemas reject judgment-shaped fields, nonfinite numbers, duplicate keys, and
noncanonical payloads. Every record carries the full prompt and the verbatim source span
$s_i$, and heavy artifacts are content-addressed by path and byte hash. The schema thus
guarantees two things: a specialist cannot emit the final prompt-satisfaction label, and
accepted evidence is attributable and replayable. It cannot make a learned measurement
unbiased, nor detect a well-formed payload that is wrong.

\subsubsection{Three-valued verification and aggregation}
\label{sec:semantics}
For a completed trace, every sub-check resolves in
\[
\mathbb{V}=\{\mathsf{S},\mathsf{C},\mathsf{U}\},
\]
standing for \supported{}, \contradicted{}, and \unknownv{}. A typed resolver
$\psi_q$ maps measured parent payloads to a sub-check state; it is deterministic
for count, timing, track, transcription, and other explicit predicates, and is
learned only for the semantic sub-checks named above:
\begin{equation}
z_q
=
\begin{cases}
\psi_q(\{\mu_j:a_j\in\operatorname{Pa}(q)\}),
  & \sigma_j=\mathtt{measured}\ \text{for all required }a_j,\\
\mathsf{U},
  & \text{a required action abstained or produced no usable evidence},\\
\bot_{\mathrm{infra}},
  & \text{a required action has a terminal infrastructure error}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:evidence-resolution}
\end{equation}
Learned semantic checks use an asymmetric gate: they may contradict only when
a positively contradicting feature is visible; unresolved visual evidence,
unparseable output, or missing modality yields $\mathsf{U}$. This avoids turning
failure of an instrument into an accusation about the video.

Negation is applied to the positive sub-check state, never by asking a tool to
measure a negative quantity:
\begin{equation}
\neg_3\mathsf{S}=\mathsf{C},
\qquad
\neg_3\mathsf{C}=\mathsf{S},
\qquad
\neg_3\mathsf{U}=\mathsf{U}.
\label{eq:three-valued-negation}
\end{equation}
Let $K_i=\{q\in\mathcal Q:b(q)=i\}$ be the validated checks for claim $c_i$,
after applying \cref{eq:three-valued-negation} where required. Claim and clip
composition are
\begin{equation}
y_i=
\begin{cases}
\mathsf{C}, & \exists q\in K_i:z_q=\mathsf{C},\\
\mathsf{S}, & \forall q\in K_i:z_q=\mathsf{S},\\
\mathsf{U}, & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\qquad
Y=
\begin{cases}
\bot_{\mathrm{infra}}, & \text{the trace terminates with an infrastructure error},\\
\textsc{Implausible}, & \exists i:y_i=\mathsf{C},\\
\textsc{Plausible}, & N_p\geq1\ \land\ \forall i:y_i=\mathsf{S},\\
\textsc{Abstain}, & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:claim-clip-rollup}
\end{equation}
The cases are ordered: one contradiction gives an implausible clip, plausible requires every
planned claim supported, and a trace with no contradiction but at least one unknown abstains.
\textsc{Plausible} is therefore a statement about the claims the plan executed.

\paragraph{Conflict resolution between two verifiers.}
\label{sec:review}
A claim the video-aware verifier passes may be re-examined by a stronger
reviewing model, and that model's verdict stands: where it overrules a pass, the
claim resolves to \contradicted{} rather than to \unknownv{}. Formally, for a claim
whose sub-checks would roll up to $\mathsf{S}$ under \cref{eq:claim-clip-rollup},
the reviewer's state replaces $y_i$. Both verdicts are kept in the record, so a row
can be re-scored on the first verifier's alone.

The direction is asymmetric by design. The reviewer examines only claims the first
verifier passed, and never a specialist measurement, because a measurement carries
evidence that a second opinion has no way to inspect.

\paragraph{Reading on-screen text.}
A recognition engine reads frames directly to settle text claims. Asked what a sign says, a
generative model repairs what it sees, rendering a malformed \code{Stly your} as the
well-formed \code{Style your} the prompt requested and concealing the defect under test; a
recognition head returns the malformed string, which a deterministic comparison rejects. The
engine reads every frame rather than a sampled fraction, since a caption present for part of a
clip can fall between samples, and each record states the frames read.

\subsubsection{Diagnostic interface: the contradiction packet}
\label{sec:loop}
Beyond the clip state $Y$, a run emits a localized contradiction packet
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{F}(p,\mathcal V)
=
\left\{
\bigl(c_i,\{R_{j,i}\}_j,\Omega_i,B_i\bigr)
:\ y_i=\mathsf{C}
\right\},
\label{eq:refinement-packet}
\end{equation}
where $\Omega_i$ is the union of available temporal support and $B_i$ contains available box
tracks or masks. The packet is assembled from the trace that produced the verdict, so each
contradicted claim carries both the evidence records behind the conclusion and the region of
the clip in which the failure occurs: \emph{what} failed, \emph{on what evidence}, and
\emph{where}. That space--time localization is what \Cref{sec:framework} maps onto a local edit
of the generator control (\Cref{sec:framework:control}, \eqref{eq:control}).

\textbf{Closed-loop refinement is not run in this report.} The critic and the
simulation-conditioned generator are evaluated on disjoint data, and no
\PhyReAct{} packet has yet been used for preference optimization, denoising
guidance, candidate selection, or localized regeneration.
\Cref{eq:refinement-packet} defines the interface only; we make no claim that the
current generator has been optimized by the critic or that any packet has made a
generated clip more physically correct.

\subsection{Generation Experiments}\label{sec:gen-experiments}

We evaluate three questions separately from the method definition: whether geometric control
overrides prompt-implied motion, when a conditioned trajectory becomes insensitive to control
withdrawal, and how the pipeline behaves beyond the single-body probes. The first and third
analyses use the deployed generator; the release analysis is an explicitly separate
mechanism probe on a second backbone.

\subsubsection{Experimental setup and implementation}\label{sec:gen-setup}

The deployed backbone is Wan~2.2-VACE-Fun-14B, run through the
\code{WanVACEPipeline} interface. Each appearance prompt is paired with the same negative prompt
(\emph{cartoon, CGI, render, grayscale, flat shading}), and the optional appearance reference is
unset. Sampling is deterministic given the recorded seed. The two expert stacks are
CPU-offloaded because their weights exceed one 80\,GB accelerator.

For three-dimensional scenes, MuJoCo integrates gravity at $9.81\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ with a
one-millisecond step, a fourth-order integrator, and an elliptic friction cone. Each scene is
rendered to 81 frames and written at 16\,fps. Because the simulated duration varies by scene,
this render clock stretches playback by approximately $1.8\times$--$2.3\times$ in the evaluated
set: trajectory shape and event order come from the simulation, but absolute video speed does
not equal simulator time.

Generation is staged. Controls are rendered first, a 1.3B checkpoint screens each scene once,
and visibly degenerate outputs are not queued for the 14B model. The surviving scenes are then
generated with the configuration in \cref{tab:genconfig}; this screening rule is part of the
reported pipeline rather than a property of the generator.

\subsubsection{Control fidelity under prompt variation}\label{sec:gen-prompt}

What each input determines can be measured by holding one fixed and varying the other. The geometric control is the same projectile depth video throughout; the
prompt varies over three wordings that share their subject, scene, camera, material and style and
differ only in the motion clause. One states a strong horizontal traverse, one is neutral, and
one is adversarial with respect to the control, requiring the object to drop nearly straight down
and bounce in place with almost no sideways motion. Each prompt is generated under three
conditioning regimes: no control at any step, control over the first $10$ of $50$ steps, and
control throughout, at three seeds for the partial regime, two for the unconditioned one and one
for the full regime. The measured quantity is the horizontal distance the object travels as a
fraction of frame width, recovered by tracking it in the finished clip. The metric and the decision rules were registered before generation.

\subsubsection{Control release and trajectory commitment}\label{sec:gen-release}

Moving the withdrawal point locates the noise level at which the simulated trajectory becomes
fixed. These measurements were made on a second Wan2.2-derived backbone, run at $40$ UniPC steps
with a flow shift of $5.0$. It has no trained control branch: its only conditioning path encodes
a rendered source video into context tokens concatenated with the text and image tokens. That
makes the withdrawal directly available --- the source tokens are removed from the attended
sequence at a chosen step, a different operation from the additive residual injection of
\cref{eq:control-release} ---
and it is the configuration on which the schedule of \cref{eq:flow-sigma} and the expert switch
below are defined. The source render is encoded into video
tokens that are concatenated with the image and text tokens the sampler attends over, each
stream carrying a scale $\omega$. Withdrawal removes the video tokens from the sequence
outright, rather than scaling an additive residual to zero: writing $m_i\in\{0,1\}$ for their
presence at step $i$ of $S$,
\begin{equation}
m_i = \mathbb{1}\!\left[\,i < i^{*}\,\right],
\qquad
i^{*} = \operatorname{clip}\big(\operatorname{round}(f S),\, 0,\, S-1\big),
\label{eq:token-drop}
\end{equation}
with $f$ the withdrawal fraction. Steps before $i^{*}$ attend over the source tokens at the
scale $\omega$ the deployed recipe uses; from $i^{*}$ onward the sequence is identical to that of an
unconditioned generation.

Because $i^{*}$ is an index, the noise level it corresponds to depends on the schedule. The
backbone samples on the flow-matching schedule of its UniPC solver, so for $S$ steps at shift
$\kappa$ over $N$ training timesteps,
\begin{equation}
\tilde{s}_i = \frac{(S-i)\,(1 - 1/N)}{S},
\qquad
\sigma_i = \frac{\kappa\,\tilde{s}_i}{1 + (\kappa-1)\,\tilde{s}_i},
\qquad
\sigma_{\mathrm{rel}} = \sigma_{i^{*}} .
\label{eq:flow-sigma}
\end{equation}
Two runs that withdraw at the same step index therefore correspond to different noise levels
whenever $S$ or $\kappa$ differs.

The solver is multistep and forms each update from derivatives cached at previous steps, so
after a withdrawal those cached values still carry the influence of the removed tokens. One of the two variants below clears that history at
$i^{*}$ and the other leaves it in place, and the two are otherwise identical.

The control is a shaded-colour render rather than a depth render, and the scenes consist of a
single moving rigid body at one resolution and frame count, chosen because their trajectories can
be checked against a closed-form path. One of the three yields only five measurable frames after
ground contact, so the windowed comparisons rest on two, and each schedule setting carries one
seed.

Withdrawing the conditioning partway leaves the remaining steps unconditioned. Noise decreases as
sampling proceeds, so a \emph{high} $\sigma_{\mathrm{rel}}$ is an \emph{early} withdrawal and a low one
a late withdrawal; that convention is used throughout. A clip that still follows the simulated
trajectory after an early withdrawal shows the trajectory was determined before the control was
removed, so the earliest withdrawal that preserves agreement marks where the trajectory becomes
fixed.

Three runs are reported, each answering a different question. The first fixes one withdrawal
point and repeats it over $16$ seeds, giving the seed-to-seed variability. The second sweeps six
withdrawal points on one seed, giving the shape of the transition. The third re-scores a denser
sweep with an independent tracker, testing whether the effect depends on how the object is
measured. The quantities they report---retention, its two windows, and the two failure
counts---are defined next.

\paragraph{Metrics.} Each run fixes one $\sigma_{\mathrm{rel}}$ and is paired against a run at
$\sigma_{\mathrm{rel}} = 0$ sharing its simulation, control video, prompt, sampler and seed.
Release points are indexed by $\sigma_{\mathrm{rel}}$.

Both the control video and the
generated clip are tracked frame by frame, giving the object's path in each. The default tracker
locates the moving body by its colour against the background and reports the centroid of the
largest matching region; the third run below substitutes a segmentation-based tracker, which
takes the centroid of the object's predicted mask instead.

Write $p^{\mathrm{gen}}_t=(x^{\mathrm{gen}}_t,y^{\mathrm{gen}}_t)$ and
$p^{\mathrm{ctrl}}_t$ for the tracked positions at frame $t\in\{0,\ldots,T-1\}$, undefined on
frames where the tracker locates nothing, and let
$V=\{t: p^{\mathrm{gen}}_t \text{ and } p^{\mathrm{ctrl}}_t \text{ both defined}\}$. Retention
over a window $W$ is the Pearson correlation of the two coordinate series, taken separately per
axis over the frames of $W$ at which both are located,
\begin{equation}
R_x(W) \;=\; \rho\!\left(\{x^{\mathrm{gen}}_t\}_{t\in W\cap V},\,
                          \{x^{\mathrm{ctrl}}_t\}_{t\in W\cap V}\right),
\qquad
R_y(W) \;=\; \rho\!\left(\{y^{\mathrm{gen}}_t\}_{t\in W\cap V},\,
                          \{y^{\mathrm{ctrl}}_t\}_{t\in W\cap V}\right),
\label{eq:retention}
\end{equation}
reported only where $|W\cap V|$ reaches a minimum count. The intersection with $V$ is what makes
a frame the tracker misses in either video drop out of both series rather than either one alone.

The two windows are separated at the first frame of ground contact, read off the control's own
vertical path rather than set per scene. With $y^{\mathrm{ctrl}}$ measured downward and
$t_{\mathrm{apex}}=\arg\min_{t\in V} y^{\mathrm{ctrl}}_t$ the apex of the tracked arc,
\begin{equation}
t_c \;=\; \min\Big\{\, t \geq t_{\mathrm{apex}} \;:\; y^{\mathrm{ctrl}}_t \geq
   y_{\max} - \tfrac{1}{10}\big(y_{\max}-y_{\min}\big) \Big\},
\label{eq:contact}
\end{equation}
where $y_{\min}$ and $y_{\max}$ are taken over the located frames of the control. The
\emph{airborne} window is $W_{\mathrm{air}}=\{t<t_c\}$ and the \emph{after-contact} window
$W_{\mathrm{post}}=\{t\geq t_c\}$. A value of $R$ near $1$ means the generated object moved as the
simulation did. Two windows are reported, since the windowed metrics localise a disagreement that a
single whole-clip value does not.

Two failure modes are counted alongside retention. A
\emph{whole-clip disagreement} is a clip whose horizontal agreement over the full clip falls
below $0.7$. A \emph{late-window disagreement} is a clip meeting two conditions at once, whole-clip
horizontal agreement below $0.7$ and airborne horizontal agreement above $0.9$. Since the
airborne window agrees while the clip as a whole does not, the disagreement is located in the
remaining frames. Both thresholds were fixed before scoring.

\paragraph{Release-point sweep across three scenes.} Sweeping the withdrawal point across six settings on a
single seed for three scenes estimates retention as a function of withdrawal noise level
(\cref{fig:injsweep}). For the thrown ball, releasing at $\sigma = 0.952$ gives an
after-contact horizontal agreement of $-0.63$, indicating motion opposite to the simulation's, with the
airborne window still at $1.00$; releasing at $\sigma = 0.903$ or later restores the
after-contact window to $0.98$ or above, and it stays there through the latest release point
tested, $\sigma = 0.555$. The same sweep on a ball dropped onto a floor stays above the threshold at every setting:
after-contact horizontal agreement is $1.00$ at every release point including the earliest, and
the vertical agreement, the informative direction for a straight-down drop, is lowest at the
earliest release, $0.78$ at $\sigma = 0.952$, rising to $0.99$ or above from $\sigma = 0.903$
onward. A ball rolling off a ramp remains at $0.99$ or above at every withdrawal point in both
directions; a locatable object is present in about a third of its frames, and each of its
correlations is computed over that subset. Across all three scenes,
the horizontal component recovers at earlier withdrawal points than the vertical component.

\paragraph{Replicated withdrawal points under a different tracker.} A third run covers fewer
withdrawal points than the sweep above but replicates each of them. It takes three withdrawal
points on the thrown ball at three seeds and two classifier-free guidance settings, $18$ clips
in all, and scores them with the segmentation-based tracker rather than the colour tracker. It reproduces the
direction of the effect: airborne horizontal agreement is $1.00$ (mean over the $6$ clips at
$\sigma = 0.952$, $95\%$ CI $0.998$ to $1.000$), while after-contact horizontal agreement at
the same release point averages $0.69$ with a confidence interval spanning zero ($-0.04$ to
$1.43$, $n=6$) and is negative on one clip; at $\sigma = 0.903$ and $\sigma = 0.833$ the
after-contact window recovers to $0.99$ and $1.00$. At the latest withdrawal point the tracker locates no object on $3$ of $6$ clips; manual visual inspection of those
clips shows a ball of low contrast relative to the grass background. Clips yielding no location
are excluded, so that cell's mean and interval are computed over $3$ clips and are correspondingly
wide.

\paragraph{Location of the commitment point.} Across the runs the trajectory stops responding to
removal of the control in a narrow band of noise, in the region of $\sigma \approx 0.94$ to
$0.96$. Under \cref{eq:flow-sigma} at $S=40$ and $\kappa=5.0$ that band lies in the high-noise
portion of sampling, consistent with the critical-window
account~\cite{phasetrans2024,dynregimes2024,critwindows2024}. It also lies above the backbone's
own architectural boundary: the model runs two expert networks and switches from the high-noise
to the low-noise one when the timestep falls below $0.875\,N$~\cite{wan22dual2026}, so the motion is fixed
before the handoff occurs.

Of the six clips scored at the schedule settings, two diverge from the reference and one is
borderline. Three yield no usable track, so the band's width is not estimated. Within it the flight arc and the ground contact are not separated:
both recover at the same band as the withdrawal is moved later.

Under \cref{eq:flow-sigma} a change in $S$ or $\kappa$ moves the step index at which the band
falls while leaving the noise level unchanged, which is the reason for the coordinate adopted
here. Prior work on partial conditioning organises the process by noise level for the same
reason~\cite{sdedit2022,p2train2022}.

\paragraph{Which component recovers last.} Separating the two windows shows that the components
of the motion do not become fixed together. On the thrown ball, withdrawing at $\sigma = 0.9522$ drops after-contact agreement below the
threshold in both coordinates, horizontal to $-0.632$ and vertical to $+0.016$. Moving the withdrawal one point later, to $\sigma = 0.9025$, restores the horizontal
component to $+0.981$ while the vertical is still at $+0.260$; the vertical does not reach
$+0.894$ until $\sigma = 0.8331$ and $+0.975$ until $\sigma = 0.5552$. The ball dropped onto a floor shows the same ordering at the single
withdrawal point where its agreement falls, horizontal $+1.000$ against vertical $+0.784$ at
$\sigma = 0.9522$. A withdrawal schedule set by the horizontal component alone would
therefore be too early for the motion that remains.

Over the whole clip the thrown ball's vertical agreement is $+0.986$ against a horizontal
$+0.421$, the reverse ordering. That figure is produced by the large up-and-down excursion of the
flight, which dominates the correlation and leaves it insensitive to the rebound that follows.

\paragraph{The contact as the limiting phase.} The failure an early withdrawal produces is specific: the flight is
reproduced and the behaviour after ground contact is not, with the ball reversing direction on
$9$ of $16$ seeds at $\sigma = 0.952$. Contacts and collisions are a documented failure mode of
video generators~\cite{videophy2024}, which locates the mechanism in the strength of the model's
prior over contact. Where that prior is weak the control must remain present to override the
default it supplies: the baseline with the control applied throughout reproduces the contact
correctly on every seed, and the reversal appears only when the guidance is withdrawn early.

\paragraph{Sampling coverage and the solver reset.} Clearing the sampler's cached
derivatives changes the result by at most $0.04$ horizontally over the whole clip ($95\%$ CI
$-0.04$ to $0.13$, $n=16$), the largest per-seed difference on any window or direction
measured.

\subsubsection{Scene-library breadth and failure boundaries}\label{sec:gen-breadth}

The same pipeline applies to scenes of higher body count than the commitment analysis covers. It has produced $1{,}314$ scenes across $66$ kinds, where a \emph{kind} is a scene
template and a \emph{scene} is one parameter setting of a kind, simulated once and generated
once. Before any photorealistic render, $652$ of the simulations were inspected and $591$ of
those $652$ judged to show the intended physical behaviour clearly enough to generate from. The
inspection covered simulator output; generated clips were not scored in it.

A single authoring, simulation and conditioning path covers body counts from one to several
dozen and several classes of physics. \Cref{fig:scenebreadth} shows twelve of them as
generated frames: cloth draping and crumpling, a flag in wind, a swinging rope and a hanging
chain, grains pouring into a heap and draining through a funnel, a ball dropped into a pit of
balls, a loaded boat, a cascade down a slope, toppling dominoes and a chaotic two-arm linkage.
The body counts are the scenes' own parameters, reaching $34$ small bodies for the pouring heap,
$30$ for the funnel, $20$ rigid slabs for the dominoes, $9$ in contact for the ball pit and $6$
on the ramp for the cascade. \Cref{fig:scenepairs} shows three of them as depth control and generated clip at matched
timestamps; the three are those whose depth control stays distinguishable from its background
throughout, since an object at rest on the floor takes the floor's grey and leaves the control
blank.

Fidelity does not extend uniformly across that range. On a small set of scenes assessed by
manual visual inspection together with the colour tracker of \cref{sec:gen-release},
single-object scenes reproduce the simulated trajectory, with the generated object following the
control throughout (\cref{fig:gencases}); a multi-object interaction such as a billiards break
does not, and neither the deployed backbone nor the alternates tried against it produced a
collision in which every body moved as the simulation specified. The commitment point for
multi-body motion is likewise unmeasured, since the colour tracker cannot follow a scatter as a
single path.

Every scene in the library is produced by the same rigid-body engine, and the deformable and
granular kinds are built from that engine's particle-and-link primitives: cloth is a sheet of
particles joined by springs, a rope or chain is a short row of linked segments, sand and marbles
are many small solid bodies in contact, and floating is a buoyancy force on a solid body in a
still fluid volume. The library therefore covers these primitives and not fluid, smoke or
finite-element soft-body solves.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol and Measurement}\label{sec:eval}
\paragraph{Target and reference standard.} The evaluation core of \cref{sec:dataset} contains
$149$ clips carrying $304$ annotated flaws. These flaws are the reference standard, under the
conventions of \cref{sec:bench-spec}, and the denominator for all core recall analyses unless
stated otherwise; the evaluation measures agreement with the annotated flaws rather than
detection of every defect present in a clip. Because these clips were inspected during system
development, the core results describe performance on development data; the held-out
evaluation is reported in \cref{sec:physion}. Dashes in tables are defined in each table's
caption.

\paragraph{Implementation.}
Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct~\cite{qwen3vl2025}, served in BF16 without
quantization and with tensor parallelism across two GPUs, is used for both the text-only
planner and the video-aware semantic verifier. These are separate calls: the
planner receives the prompt and claim state but no frames, while the verifier
receives densely decoded, timestamped frames and a single declared predicate.
The evaluated run uses frozen specialist revisions and the backends listed in
\cref{tab:arms}.

\paragraph{Dense-frame requirement.} All evaluated video-aware components receive video
through the logged dense-input path. Individual measurements may operate on a recorded
subset of the decoded frames; measurements produced from a sparse-input path are excluded.
A rerun is included only if it uses this dense input path, the same frozen evaluation
target, BF16 model inference, and pinned tool revisions.

\paragraph{Cost and uncertainty.} A detector call, a tracker call and a full vision-language
judgement do not cost the same, so cost is reported as model and tool calls per clip rather than
as wall-clock time. Uncertainty is estimated by resampling whole clips, because two flaws in one
clip are not independent.

\paragraph{Planner validity.}\label{sec:planner-gaps}
Before end-to-end evaluation, the prompt-only planning stage was inspected without video or
tool access. For $233$ claims from $22$ annotated clips, the planner produced a valid,
compilable plan for every claim, using one or two planner calls per clip; $27$ claims
($11.6\%$) contained at least one specialist action that survived validation, compared with
$2.3\%$ under an earlier tabular plan format.

\paragraph{Diagnostic routing coverage.} On diagnostic sets composed of claims that require
quantitative measurement, the routing rate was $19/30$ pooled across counting, spatial, and
ordering claims, and $10/10$ for synthetic counting prompts. These diagnostics test routing
and syntax; they are not estimates of verdict accuracy or coverage on the evaluation core.

\paragraph{Unsupported predicates and fallback handling.} The supported predicate vocabulary
lacks an ``on top of'' spatial predicate and rejects negated quantitative measurements;
claims using these forms are routed to the generalist fallback verifier and counted as
fallback cases.

\subsubsection{Matching critic findings to annotated flaws}\label{sec:matching}
Matching is performed after critic inference and cannot affect the critic's outputs.
The unit of comparison is a pair of sentences: what the annotator wrote about the clip, and
what the critic reports the video showed. Comparing the prompt fragment against the critic's
restatement of it would instead measure agreement about the prompt, since both are assertions
of the same requirement.

Each pair is judged on meaning and assigned one of three outcomes. A \emph{whole} match
requires the two statements to assert the same defect: different wording, a different name for
the same object, and a measured quantity in place of a description all qualify, while two
different problems with the same object do not. Failing that, a \emph{partial} match requires
the critic's finding to identify at least one aspect of a compound annotated defect, which an
annotator's single sentence can span while the critic reports one aspect at a time. Pairs
meeting neither condition are non-matches.

Whole and partial pairings then form the candidate edges for maximum bipartite matching,
subject to the constraint that each finding and each annotated flaw may be used at most
once; after assignment, each flaw is reported as whole, partial, or missed. Greedy
assignment can reduce the number of matched flaws: if one flaw can be matched by either of
two findings and a second flaw can be matched by only one of them, assigning that finding
to the first flaw leaves the second unmatched. 
The matching judge is distinct from all evaluated system components. For each pair, it
initially produces three votes at temperature zero. Each unparsable reply is retried once;
if the retry remains unparsable, that vote is recorded as a matching error. Among valid
votes, disagreement is resolved by assigning non-match if any valid vote is a non-match,
otherwise partial if any valid vote is partial, and otherwise whole. If no valid vote
remains after retries, the judge produces nine new votes and assigns the majority outcome.
A run aborts rather than reporting a number if the matching judge cannot produce a usable
matching outcome for more than $2\%$ of annotated flaws.



\subsubsection{Flaw recall on the evaluation core}
\label{sec:headline}
Across the $149$ clips of the evaluation core, which carry $304$ human-annotated flaws,
\PhyReAct{} finds \textbf{228 of them ($75.0\%$)} under the protocol of \cref{sec:matching}. Of
these $228$ flaws, $191$ are whole matches, each covered completely by a single finding;
the remaining $37$ are partial matches, each consisting of a finding that addresses an
aspect of the annotated defect without covering it completely.
On $91$ of the $149$ clips, the critic finds every annotated flaw in that clip.

Two components contribute to matched findings in distinct ways. For $83$ of the $228$ found
flaws, the fallback verifier, gpt-5.6-sol, overrode a pass from the dense semantic verifier
and changed the claim state to contradiction. Separately, the on-screen-text recognizer
transcribes malformed glyphs literally rather than normalizing them to the requested
phrase, allowing string comparison to identify garbled text.

\input{tab_headline}

Broken down by the annotators' own categories (\cref{tab:headline}), recall runs from $90\%$ to
$49\%$. Camera and style ($90\%$) and on-screen text ($84\%$) have the highest observed
recall: both are evaluated using a direct measurement against an explicit target. Counting has the
lowest recall at $49\%$: as described in \cref{sec:contract}, the counting path reduces a
per-frame series to its mode, so a subject detected intermittently by the segmenter may be
reported as absent. The
physical-motion category contains seven flaws, four of which are found. Most of these seven
flaws concern deformation --- cloth that folds wrongly or a body that bends where it should
not; such defects lie outside the trajectory measurements, and given the category's size,
its recall is interpreted descriptively.

\item \emph{Dolphin.} Asserted: a leap. Measured: vertical spread, $0.65$ animal radii;

\item \emph{Bottle.} Asserted: the bottle rolls. Measured: $10.20$\,px/frame surface motion

\item \emph{Sun.} Asserted: a rise. Measured: peak departure from the starting height,

\item \emph{Text.} Asserted: the requested phrase. Measured: the recognition head returns

\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Independent measurements on one clip.} The dolphin clip carries a second
annotator sentence stating that the animal never disappears into the depths; two further
specialists independently evaluated the corresponding prompt requirement that the animal
disappear. The vertical-travel check required downward motion but measured a maximum
displacement of $2.07$ radii upward, corresponding to a net image-coordinate displacement of $-317$\,px
over $37$ tracked frames, where negative vertical displacement denotes upward motion. The persistence check required the dolphin to vanish and found it on
$121$ of $121$ frames, with no gap of three frames or more. Both specialist measurements
independently contradict that prompt requirement.

\paragraph{Measurement coverage.} The record also states the temporal coverage of each
measurement. In the banner case the recognition head read $25$ of that clip's $241$ frames at
a stride of ten, and the record states both figures. A transcript stable across those $25$
frames is strong evidence about a banner present throughout the clip; a caption appearing
only briefly could fall between the frames sampled.

\paragraph{Specialist abstention.} In the evaluated run of \cref{tab:headline}, physical
specialists were called on $227$ checks, abstaining on $166$ and contradicting the prompt
on $61$; each abstention carries the reason it occurred. The
most common abstention occurred when no candidate grounding passed screening: the segmenter
produced no usable track for the named subject. Groundings are screened by a semantic guard,
shown one frame with the candidate boxes drawn on it and asked only whether those regions
correspond to the named subject. On a clip requesting seagulls scattering in the wake of
fighter jets, the boxes offered for ``the seagulls'' contained the jets, and the guard
discarded them. The check then yields \unknownv{}, separating a failure to find the subject
from a violation by it.

\subsubsection{Scope and interpretation}
The evaluations use distinct settings and should be interpreted separately. Core recall
uses recorded annotations on development data, where an unrecorded defect cannot be scored
either way; the question-decomposition baseline was specified after inspection of critic
outputs; the VideoPhy-2 analysis measures clip-level agreement with human ratings; and the

\section{Framework: Learning as State Optimization}\label{sec:framework}
% =====================================================================

The preceding sections described three artifacts: a
physics-guided generator (\Cref{sec:generation}), a $1{,}500$-clip human-authored
defect benchmark (\Cref{sec:dataset}), and a physically grounded, tool-using
critic whose plan-based adjudication we measure in \Cref{sec:eval}. This section states the
abstraction that ties them together and the form the framework is built toward. Our thesis is that a physical-reasoning
agent should keep everything it has learned in one
\emph{human-readable, typed, provenance-carrying state}, and should improve by
\emph{optimizing that state}. Every improvement
is then an edit to an object a person can read, version, and veto, and the arc of
the framework culminates in a system that evolves itself without ever touching a
weight.

\Cref{sec:experiments} reports experiments; the remainder of this section
sets out the framework and the roadmap. Because all learning is state optimization on a readable
$S$, that boundary falls inside the formalism itself, and \Cref{tab:builtvsroadmap} marks it.

\subsection{From ReAct and agent memory to a physical agent with typed state}
\label{sec:framework:genealogy}

Agentic reasoning in the ReAct lineage interleaves \emph{reason}, \emph{act}, and
\emph{observe} steps, letting a model call external tools and condition on their
returns~\cite{react2023,reflexion2023,critic2024}. Program-of-thought variants
push this further: the chain of thought is emitted \emph{as a program} that
composes tool calls~\cite{rewoo2023,llmcompiler2024,vipergpt2023,visprog2023,%
toolformer2023,hugginggpt2023}. Orthogonally, a fast-growing agent-memory
literature asks where an agent's accumulated experience should live---episodic
transcripts, distilled semantic priors, or reusable
procedures~\cite{contexttraining2026,reasoningbank2026,explicitmem2026,%
plugmem2026,vilomem2026,amem2025,agentmemsurvey2024,m3agent2025,hindsight2026,%
aimeetsbrain2026}. \PhyReAct{} combines these two lines and changes the \emph{carrier} of memory: where these
agents store free-text notes or latent vectors, it stores structured physical-verification traces
with full provenance. Auditability follows from that choice, and it is the throughline of the
framework below.

\subsection{The unifying abstraction}
\label{sec:framework:abstraction}

\PhyReAct{} maintains a single, human-readable \emph{agent state}
\begin{equation}
  S \;=\; (\mathcal{O},\; M,\; K),
  \label{eq:state}
\end{equation}
and it improves by optimizing $S$. The reasoning model and the low-level physical
experts are frozen, so what the system accumulates is held in $S$, which is inspectable,
diffable, versionable, and revertible. ``Training'' and
``inference'' are one readable procedure applied in two directions: one
map reads $S$ to act, and channels write to $S$ (or to the generator) to improve.

\begin{description}
  \item[$\mathcal{O}$ --- operator library (procedural memory).] A set of reusable,
    already-compiled measurement operators. Each operator $o\in\mathcal{O}$ is a closure
    mapping a video and typed arguments to a physical measurement, and it carries
    an explicit \emph{cost node} $c(o)$ recording its spending (compute, expert
    calls, latency). $\mathcal{O}$ grows monotonically as recurring reasoning
    sub-procedures are packaged into named operators.
  \item[$M$ --- experience library (episodic + semantic memory).] Versioned,
    human-readable priors distilled from past traces: nearest-neighbor
    \emph{plans} for similar prompts and \emph{physics-failure priors} over
    common defect modes. $M$ keeps \emph{evidence} (what was measured) separate
    from \emph{belief} (what we now expect): evidence is retained as
    recorded, while belief is defeasible and is overridden by fresh measurement.
  \item[$K$ --- external knowledge channel.] Retrieved external or social
    knowledge (physical facts, domain conventions, human corrections) admitted
    only through a candidate-and-pruning discipline. $K$ is the deliberate
    \emph{open} port that keeps the internal improvement loop from closing on
    itself (\Cref{sec:selfevolve}).
\end{description}

The rest of the framework consists of two kinds of maps over this state: a
single-episode \emph{read} operator that consumes $S$ and emits an auditable
trace (\Cref{sec:framework:episode}), and \emph{write} channels that update the
generator and $S$ from that trace. The six design pillars of the paper are
recovered as special cases of read-vs-write over $S$: P1/P2 and the read side of
P4 are ``read $S$ to produce a trace''; P3 and P6 are ``write $S$''; P5 is
``write the generator''; and the supervisory side of P4 wraps the writes.

\subsection{The single-episode operator (read path): physicalized ReAct as a program over \texorpdfstring{$\mathcal{O}$}{O}}
\label{sec:framework:episode}

Given a prompt--video pair $(p, V)$, one episode is a deterministic pipeline that
only \emph{reads} $S$:
\begin{align}
  \text{retrieve:}    &\quad (\pi_p, m_p, k_p) = r(S, p), \label{eq:retrieve}\\
  \text{compile:}     &\quad (\mathcal{C}, G) = \mathrm{Reason}(p, V \mid \pi_p, m_p, k_p), \label{eq:compile}\\
  \text{act--observe:}&\quad R = \mathrm{Exec}_{\mathcal{O}}(G, V), \label{eq:exec}\\
  \text{adjudicate:}  &\quad (Y, \mathcal{F}) = \Phi(\mathcal{C}, R), \label{eq:adjudicate}\\
  \text{emit:}        &\quad T = (\mathcal{C}, G, R, Y, \mathcal{F}). \label{eq:trace}
\end{align}
These maps are the abstract form of the critic of \cref{sec:critic}: \eqref{eq:compile}
is the claim-and-plan synthesis of \cref{eq:claims-and-plan}, \eqref{eq:exec} the scoped
execution of \cref{eq:scoped-execution}, and \eqref{eq:adjudicate} the roll-up and
packet of \cref{eq:claim-clip-rollup,eq:refinement-packet}.
Here $\mathcal{C}$ is a set of \emph{typed physical obligations}---explicit,
machine-checkable statements of what the video must satisfy to honor the prompt
(object presence and count, contact and support, trajectory continuity, depth
ordering, on-screen text, audio events). $G$ is a \emph{program over $\mathcal{O}$}:
reasoning takes the form of a composition of operator calls
that the model writes and the runtime executes, in the tradition of
program-of-thought and modular visual
reasoning~\cite{visprog2023,vipergpt2023,rewoo2023,llmcompiler2024}. Executing $G$
drives the classic reason$\rightarrow$act$\rightarrow$observe loop of ReAct-style
agents~\cite{react2023}, but every \texttt{act} is a call into a \emph{frozen}
physical expert operator---promptable segmentation and tracking~\cite{sam3_2025},
counting over its instance identities, the typed physical measurements taken over those
tracks, monocular depth, OCR, and audio event
detection~\cite{flexsed2025}---registered in $\mathcal{O}$. This is the sense in which
\PhyReAct{} specializes ReAct to physical measurement: it inherits the control-flow harness from
mainstream tool-using agents~\cite{toolformer2023,hugginggpt2023} and specializes
it with explicit physical obligations and physical-measurement operators
\textbf{(P1)}. This general form is instantiated concretely by the critic of
\Cref{sec:critic}; \Cref{eq:verdict} below is the same three-valued verdict used
there.

Each operator returns a measurement together with a \emph{gate} on its own scope:
when an operator's preconditions are not met (out-of-scope object, untrackable
occlusion, no audio track), it emits \abstain{}.
The adjudicator $\Phi$ in \eqref{eq:adjudicate} is a \emph{deterministic} roll-up
from evidence $R$ to a per-obligation verdict in the three-valued codomain
$\mathbb{V}=\{\mathsf{S},\mathsf{C},\mathsf{U}\}$ of \cref{sec:critic},
\begin{equation}
  Y \;\in\; \mathbb{V}^{|\mathcal{C}|},
  \label{eq:verdict}
\end{equation}
one entry per obligation (\supported{}, \contradicted{}, or \unknownv{}),
plus a \emph{contradiction packet} $\mathcal{F}$ that localizes, for each unmet obligation,
which measurement contradicts it and where. \unknownv{}, and \abstain{} for out-of-scope
obligations, are first-class outcomes of the codomain, so declining to certify is available to
the critic as a verdict in its own right. The
emitted trace $T=(\mathcal{C},G,R,Y,\mathcal{F})$ carries full provenance---every element of $Y$ is
back-traceable to the operator calls in $G$ and the measurements in $R$.

\paragraph{CoT-as-program and cost nodes \textnormal{(P2)}.} Because $G$ is a
program, recurring sub-procedures (e.g.\ ``verify a bounce $=$ track the object,
detect the contact frame, check the pre/post vertical-velocity sign flip'') can
be packaged, named, and re-registered as new operators in $\mathcal{O}$. Each operator
carries its cost node $c(o)$, so a program has a well-defined budget
$c(G)=\sum_{o\in G} c(o)$ and the reasoner can trade breadth of measurement
against spending. Over many episodes $\mathcal{O}$ scales up into a growing procedural
memory, and reasoning increasingly becomes \emph{composition of trusted
operators} rather than re-derivation from scratch. What is instantiated today is
a fixed operator set $\mathcal{O}_0$ and a fixed compiler; the \emph{scaling-up} of $\mathcal{O}$
via distillation is roadmap (\Cref{sec:framework:builtvsroadmap}).

\subsection{The outward channel: verification-guided refinement \textnormal{(P5)}}
\label{sec:framework:control}

The read path produces, for a failed clip, a localized contradiction packet $\mathcal{F}$
(defined in \Cref{sec:loop}). The outward
\emph{control channel} maps $\mathcal{F}$ into a \emph{local edit $\Delta_{\mathrm{ctrl}}$ of the
generator's control signal} and regenerates:
\begin{equation}
  V' \;=\; \mathrm{Gen}\!\big(p,\; \delta(\mathcal{F})\big),
  \qquad \delta:\ \mathcal{F} \;\longmapsto\; \Delta_{\mathrm{ctrl}},
  \label{eq:control}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{Gen}$ is the physics-guided pipeline of \Cref{sec:generation}
(MuJoCo simulation~\cite{todorov2012mujoco} driving a Wan~2.2 video
model~\cite{wan2025} through VACE control conditioning~\cite{vace2025}), and
$\delta(\mathcal{F})$ is an edit to the simulation and control inputs addressing the violated obligation.
Since the control signal is a rendered field over space and time, a contradiction localized in
both maps onto a local edit of the conditioning stream. The critic here is independent and
frozen, so the grading signal does not originate in the generator being graded, unlike
self-evaluating refinement loops~\cite{mavis2025} and verification-guided
repair~\cite{neuse2025}. Both endpoints of \eqref{eq:control} are built
(\Cref{sec:generation,sec:eval}); the map $\delta$ and the closed loop are roadmap.

\subsection{Auditability of the state and the trace}
\label{sec:framework:audit}

Auditability follows from the formalism. The learned object is the readable state $S$ and every
conclusion is a deterministic roll-up $\Phi$ over provenance-carrying measurements, so any verdict
$Y$ replays back to the operator calls in $G$ and the measurements in $R$, and any state change
$S\!\rightarrow\!S'$ is a human-readable diff of $\mathcal{O}$, $M$, or $K$. Weight-free self-evolution and
its human supervision both rest on that property: the reviewer inspects readable objects rather
than gradients.

Auditability determines what a verdict can be taken to mean. A \supported{} verdict is a critic judgment, where the
ground truth in this paper is human annotation. Determinism in the roll-up leaves evaluator drift
in the underlying operators and the compiler untouched. A prompt surviving the checks is
\emph{plausible} with respect to the obligations tested, and a static critic improving says
nothing about the generator.

\subsection{Weight-free context-training self-evolution}
\label{sec:selfevolve}

The framework's inward channel provides: a critic that improves itself without
updating a weight. Everything above---a readable state, a program over
operators, provenance traces, an independent verdict---exists so that the
system's own experience can be written into $S$ under a guard. This inward \emph{context-training} channel
\textbf{(P3)} is the last of the framework's components to be instantiated.

Given a trace $T$ and feedback $\phi$ (human labels, downstream outcomes, or a
later, more complete measurement), we distill \emph{candidate} state edits and
accept them under a strict guard:
\begin{align}
  (\Delta\mathcal{O}, \Delta M, \Delta K) &= \mathrm{Distill}(T, \phi), \label{eq:distill}\\
  S' &= \mathrm{Accept}\big(S,\, \Delta\mathcal{O}, \Delta M, \Delta K\big). \label{eq:accept}
\end{align}
Both successful and failed episodes are distilled (hindsight over
traces~\cite{hindsight2026,reasoningbank2026}): a success may add a plan to $M$ or
an operator to $\mathcal{O}$; a failure may add a physics-failure prior or a scope restriction.
This form of self-evolution leaves weights unchanged, in the spirit of
context-distillation and experience-memory
agents~\cite{contexttraining2026,reasoningbank2026,reflexion2023,explicitmem2026,%
amem2025,agentmemsurvey2024,vilomem2026}, and it treats deployment-time adaptation
as \emph{learning as state optimization}. Acceptance is governed by
\begin{equation}
  S' =
  \begin{cases}
    S \oplus \Delta, & \text{if } U_{\mathrm{val}}(S\oplus\Delta) > U_{\mathrm{val}}(S),\\[2pt]
    S, & \text{otherwise (do-nothing elitism),}
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:elitism}
\end{equation}
where $U_{\mathrm{val}}$ is a \emph{held-out proxy} score, $\Delta=(\Delta\mathcal{O},\Delta M,\Delta K)$
is a distilled candidate edit from \eqref{eq:distill}, and $S\oplus\Delta$ applies that edit
component-wise to the state, appending the operator, plan and knowledge deltas to
$\mathcal{O}$, $M$ and $K$ respectively. Candidates that do not strictly improve the proxy are
pruned, and when none improves it the state is left unchanged. That default makes the proxy
monotone across updates, bounding how far a distilled candidate can degrade $S$.

\paragraph{Admitting external knowledge \textnormal{(P6)}.}
A system whose only inputs are its own traces---CoT $\rightarrow$ feedback
$\rightarrow$ context $\rightarrow$ CoT---is a \emph{closed} loop that can amplify
its own biases: distilled priors condition future reasoning, whose traces distill
into more priors. The external knowledge channel $K$ is the opening provided for this.
\PhyReAct{} may issue retrieval or ask for external or social knowledge (physical
facts, human corrections, domain conventions) and admit it into $K$ under the same
candidate-and-pruning discipline as \eqref{eq:elitism}: a retrieved candidate is admitted only when it improves the held-out proxy, and $K$-sourced facts carry a provenance tag distinct from
distilled beliefs, so an admission can be reverted. External knowledge therefore enters under the
same elitism that governs $S$.

Every prior distilled into $M$ is \emph{critic-derived}: a summary of what \PhyReAct{}'s own
verification loop concluded rather than verified truth. The external channel $K$ is roadmap. The
inward channel is instantiated, and \cref{sec:selfevolve:measured} measures it.

\subsection{Distilling lessons from recorded misses}
\label{sec:selfevolve:measured}

We run \eqref{eq:distill} over episodes in which a plan missed a flaw a human annotator recorded.
$\mathrm{Distill}$ reads the trace and the miss and writes a lesson naming a property of the claim
and the action it licenses --- \emph{when a prompt specifies a singular entity or exact quantity,
create a separate cardinality check and verify that count across the beginning, middle and end} ---
and a curation pass groups the candidates into $33$ \emph{families} of equivalent advice and keeps
one lesson from each. Those $33$ lessons are appended to the planner's rubric; a family is the
group a kept lesson stands for, and its name is what the analysis below uses to say which kind of
defect a lesson is about. Only $M$ changes: the operator library $\mathcal{O}$ and the external channel
$K$ are untouched, so $S' = (\mathcal{O}, M', K)$.

Two aspects of the implementation depart from the formalism above. All $33$ lessons are supplied on
every clip. We built the selective form as well --- one relevance call per lesson--claim pair --- and
it is the more expensive of the two. Choosing among the $33$ lessons costs $132$ model calls for a
four-claim prompt and $495$ for a fifteen-claim one, whereas supplying all of them costs no model
calls and adds $9$\,kB to a planner prompt already $17.8$\,kB long.

The acceptance gate \eqref{eq:elitism} is also not applied. $U_{\mathrm{val}}$ is defined on rules
that have appeared in a run, so it cannot score a rule written from a fresh miss. Applied as a
diagnostic to $80$ earlier candidate rules over a ten-clip decision set, it admitted none: $39$ did
not meet its minimum-evidence criterion, and $41$ never triggered on a clip in that set. The
intervention evaluated below is therefore the curated set of $33$ lessons, without the gate.

\paragraph{Protocol.} Lessons are distilled from a pool of experience clips and tested on clips held
out from it. Excluding every clip that shares an identifier with a clip in the experience pool
leaves $363$ candidates; we then exclude the $57$ whose prompt restates the prompt of an experience clip,
so that no lesson is evaluated on the prompt it was written from. The evaluation set is the
remaining $306$ clips and the $502$ flaws they carry, and every condition is scored on those same
flaws. The untaught condition loads an empty lesson file through the same retrieval path. All are
scored by the matching protocol of \cref{sec:matching}, under which a flaw counts as \emph{found}
when the critic's findings cover it wholly or in part, the same criterion as \cref{tab:headline}.

Varying how much experience the lessons are distilled from turns the comparison into a curve. Five
conditions are run: no lessons, then $8$, $13$, $19$ and $33$ lessons distilled from $25$, $50$,
$75$ and $117$ experience clips. Each pool is a prefix of the next, so the lesson sets are nested
--- every set is the previous one plus the families the additional experience produced, with none
revised away --- and the conditions differ only in how much experience was available to distil
from.

\paragraph{Result.} With all $33$ lessons the planner finds $\mathbf{375}$ of the $502$ flaws
($\mathbf{74.7\%}$) against $340$ untaught ($67.7\%$). Relative to the untaught condition, $62$
flaws switch from missed to found and $27$ switch the other way, a net $+35$ at
$p = 3\!\times\!10^{-4}$ under a paired sign test.

Distilling from more experience does not extend that gain. The four taught conditions find $375$,
$377$, $374$ and $375$ flaws (\cref{fig:iclaggregate}); each exceeds the untaught condition by $+34$
to $+37$ at $p < 10^{-3}$, and no two of them differ by more than $3$ flaws, with every pairwise
sign test returning $p \geq 0.8$. Comparing the smallest and largest lesson sets directly, $36$
flaws move each way for a net of zero. The gain is already present with lessons distilled from $25$
clips and does not increase across the larger experience pools. Before the prompt filter,
on $363$ clips and $626$ flaws, the five conditions find $428$, $463$, $470$, $473$ and $465$:
every taught condition again exceeds the untaught one, and the taught conditions again do not
separate from each other, spanning $10$ flaws with $p \geq 0.3$ for every pairwise comparison
among them.

\paragraph{What the additional experience changes.} Although the later lessons do not change the
total number of flaws found, they change which flaws are found. Comparing the $33$-lesson
condition against the $8$-lesson one directly, $36$ flaws move each way. Of the $36$ it newly
finds, $26$ were missed in the untaught condition too and $10$ were found untaught but missed at
$8$ lessons; of the $36$ it no longer finds, $19$ were untaught misses the smaller set had
recovered and $17$ were found in both the untaught and $8$-lesson conditions. Distilling from a
further $92$ clips therefore changes which individual flaws are found while leaving the total
unchanged.

\paragraph{Effect by flaw category.} Because the conditions score the
same flaws, the informative quantity is the paired change, and its interval comes from the flaws
that switched status (\cref{tab:iclcategory}). Taking the untaught and $33$-lesson conditions, two
categories have a $95\%$ interval excluding zero. \emph{Action or event occurs}, the largest
category at $211$ flaws, gains $17$ of them, a rise of $8.1$ percentage points with a $95\%$
interval of $[+2, +12]$ points; \emph{on-screen text} gains $8$ of $73$, a rise of $11.0$ points
with $[+2, +15]$. Every other interval contains
zero. Reading the smaller movements as rates overstates them: the $+7$ points for camera and style
are one flaw of $14$, and the $+16$ for counting three of $19$. The number of flaws found rises in both of
the severity grades that carry the bulk of the set, by $19$ of $288$ at severity~$3$ ($p = 0.01$)
and by $13$ of $163$ at severity~$4$ ($p = 0.03$); the remaining $51$ flaws sit at grades $1$, $2$
and $5$.

\paragraph{Where the lessons point.} All eight families induced from the first $25$ clips concern
actions and the roles within them: decomposing an action into its parts, verifying that the
asserted action occurred, checking the manner of a motion, separating the participants of a
transfer, attributing a subject correctly, preserving a reference frame, reconciling checks that
disagree, and providing a fallback when a measurement fails. None mentions text, counting or
spatial relations. In the $8$-lesson condition \emph{action or event occurs} gains $23$ flaws and
\emph{on-screen text} $10$, together $33$ of that condition's $+35$.

\Cref{tab:iclcategory} assigns a family to a flaw category when the family's name contains a word
that names it, and to \emph{action or event occurs} otherwise; on that rule seven of these eight
names go to action or event occurs and the eighth, \emph{subject attribution error}, to object
identity. The later $25$ families name their targets more narrowly: $13$ again go to action or
event occurs --- giving the $20$ the table records --- three to order and timing, three to object
identity, two to counting, two to attributes, one to spatial relations and one to on-screen text.
Between the $8$- and $33$-lesson conditions the six non-action categories
the later families name move by $+1$ flaw for order and timing, $0$ for object identity, $+3$
for counting, $+3$ for attributes, $-1$ for spatial relations and $-2$ for on-screen text: a net of
$+4$. Action or event occurs, which draws $13$ of the $25$, falls $6$.

Category gains are therefore not consistently aligned with the lesson names. Nothing among the first eight families
mentions text, yet on-screen text gains $10$ of $73$ flaws at that condition, the largest gain of
any category in percentage points; and the later families, which do name six categories between
them, move those categories by $+4$ flaws in total. On-screen text is the clearest example in both
directions: it gains ten flaws before any lesson refers to text, and loses two after one does. This
pattern is consistent with the first lessons supplying a general instruction to separate a compound
requirement into checkable parts, one that applies to prompts well outside the categories the
lessons were written from. The plan measurements below assess that interpretation.

\paragraph{Effect of the lessons on the plan.} A lesson names a property of a claim and the check
it licenses, which suggests the lessons split one claim into several checks. That is the smaller of
the two movements they produce. Between the untaught and $33$-lesson conditions, checks per claim
rise from $1.13$ to $1.18$, while the number of typed physical claims the planner compiles from the
prompt rises from $2{,}905$ to $5{,}106$ over the $306$ evaluation clips, or $9.5$ to $16.7$ per
clip. Each lesson is a conditional instruction of the form \emph{when a prompt specifies X}, and
the change is concentrated in how many claims the prompt is compiled into rather than in how many
checks each claim receives, which is the pattern the decomposition reading predicts.

The same measurement locates where the curve flattens. Claims per clip continue to rise
across all five conditions --- $9.5$, $15.1$, $16.0$, $16.1$ and $16.7$ --- so the later lessons go
on changing the plan, but the number of flaws found does not rise with them. On-screen text shows the pattern at
clip level. Of the $56$ evaluation clips carrying a text flaw, the number receiving at least one
text check does not rise: $41$ untaught against $40$ with $33$ lessons. The two sets overlap on
$37$ clips, with three checked only when taught and four only when untaught. The number of text
checks written across those clips rises from $81$ to $136$, and the $8$-lesson condition already
produces $114$ of them. The lessons therefore add checks without widening the set of clips on which
text is checked at all, and $25$ experience clips supply $33$ of the $55$ added.

\subsection{Human oversight}
\label{sec:framework:human}

A human is kept in the loop at a few specific gates: a
reviewer may audit a trace $T$, veto or edit a proposed state update in
\eqref{eq:accept}, and approve whether a refined generation \eqref{eq:control} or
a new state $S'$ is deployed. Human feedback also enters $\mathrm{Distill}$ as a
first-class signal $\phi$. Because updates are readable diffs of $S$, oversight consists of
reading and approving them. The reviewing tooling and deployment protocol are roadmap; the
artifacts they act on --- traces, three-valued state, provenance --- exist today.

\subsection{Built and roadmap components}
\label{sec:framework:builtvsroadmap}

Because all learning is state optimization on a readable $S$, we can mark
built-vs-roadmap directly on the formalism, and each pillar is one component of the
same loop (\Cref{tab:builtvsroadmap}). \textbf{Instantiated
today (Phase~0):} the frozen generator pipeline $\mathrm{Gen}$
(\Cref{sec:generation}); the $1{,}500$-clip defect benchmark that exercises the
read path (\Cref{sec:dataset}); the episode read operator
\eqref{eq:retrieve}--\eqref{eq:trace} with a fixed operator set $\mathcal{O}_0$, typed
obligations $C$, three-valued deterministic adjudication $\Phi$, and provenance
traces $T$; the flaw-level measurement in \Cref{sec:eval} against human labels
($228$ of $304$ for \PhyReAct{}, $164$ for question decomposition, recall-only, on the development core, which is
not held out); and the inward distillation of
$M$ via \eqref{eq:distill}, measured on a held-out split in
\cref{sec:selfevolve:measured} ($375$ of $502$ against $340$ untaught).
\textbf{Roadmap (Phase~1+):} the
outward control map $\delta$ and the closed critic$\rightarrow$generator loop
\eqref{eq:control}; the scaling-up of $\mathcal{O}$; the acceptance gate \eqref{eq:elitism}

knowledge channel $K$; and human-in-the-loop deployment at scale. Every roadmap item is a \emph{write}

\section{An Executable Roadmap}
\label{sec:roadmap}

The framework of \Cref{sec:framework} defines one learned object, the state
$S=(\mathcal{O},M,K)$, and three maps over it: a read operator (built) and two write
channels (roadmap). We phase the work accordingly. Phase~0 is the read path and
the two frozen endpoints already instantiated in this paper; Phase~1 moves the
evaluation onto a held-out split scored for precision as well as recall;
Phases~2--6 exercise
the write paths, each with a measurable success metric and each a strict
superset of what precedes it. No number below the Phase~0 line is a result;
Phases~1+ state \emph{targets and protocols}, not outcomes.

\paragraph{Phase~0 --- Built: the read path and frozen endpoints (\emph{done}).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Delivered.} The physics-guided generator $\mathrm{Gen}$
    (MuJoCo\,$\rightarrow$\,Wan2.2-VACE, \Cref{sec:generation}); the $1500$-clip
    human-authored defect benchmark (\Cref{sec:dataset}); the episode read
    operator with fixed $\mathcal{O}_0$, typed obligations $C$, three-valued
    deterministic adjudication $\Phi$, and provenance traces $T$
    (\Cref{sec:critic}); and the flaw-level measurement against human labels
    ($228$ of $304$ for \PhyReAct{} against $164$ for question decomposition, on
    the development core, non-held-out, \Cref{sec:eval}).
  \item \textbf{Covers.} P1 (physical-verification read path), and the read side of
    P2 (programs over $\mathcal{O}_0$) and P4 (provenance traces).
  \item \textbf{Honest limits.} Recall-only, single non-held-out split; critic
    verdicts are judgments scored against human labels, not ground truth; a
    static evaluator result is not evidence any generator improved; the two
    write channels are not yet exercised.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Phase~1 --- Held-out, precision+recall evaluation with provenance completeness.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Goal.} Promote the critic result from dev-core / recall-only to a
    held-out, precision+recall protocol, and guarantee every verdict carries a
    complete provenance trace $T$.
  \item \textbf{Needs.} A frozen held-out split of the benchmark disjoint from
    dev-core; a provenance schema for $(C,G,R,Y,F)$.
  \item \textbf{Experiments.} Plan-based vs.\ per-claim adjudication on held-out
    clips with full confusion matrices; audit that every \contradicted{} verdict
    localizes to frame-level evidence via $F$.
  \item \textbf{Success metric.} Held-out plan-based accuracy $\geq$ per-claim
    with non-degrading precision; complete provenance coverage.
  \item \textbf{Risk.} Held-out gap reveals dev-core overfitting; the
    three-valued codomain inflates \unknownv{} to dodge hard cases --- mitigate
    by reporting the \unknownv{} rate alongside accuracy.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Phase~2 --- Operator library with cost accounting (P2).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Goal.} Turn fixed $\mathcal{O}_0$ into a growing, versioned $\mathcal{O}$ whose
    operators carry cost nodes $c(o)$ and whose programs have measurable budgets
    $c(G)$.
  \item \textbf{Needs.} An operator registry with provenance and versioning; a
    compiler that detects recurring $G$-sub-programs and promotes them into named
    operators; per-operator cost instrumentation wired into $\Phi$.
  \item \textbf{Experiments.} Track the coverage/cost frontier as $|\mathcal{O}|$ grows;
    ablate frozen $\mathcal{O}_0$ vs.\ promoted $\mathcal{O}$ at matched budget.
  \item \textbf{Success metric.} A Pareto improvement in obligation coverage
    per unit cost over the Phase~0 fixed pipeline at equal or lower $c(G)$.
  \item \textbf{Risk.} Library bloat and stale operators; promoted operators
    encode clip-specific shortcuts --- mitigate with usage-frequency thresholds
    and held-out re-validation before promotion.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Phase~3 --- Context-training self-evolution of $M$ (P3).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Goal.} Distill traces (success and failure) into human-readable,
    versionable priors in $M$ --- nearest-neighbor plans and physics-failure
    priors --- accepted under held-out proxy pruning with do-nothing elitism
    \eqref{eq:elitism}, keeping evidence separate from belief.
  \item \textbf{Needs.} A distillation function $\mathrm{Distill}$; a held-out
    validation split $U_{\mathrm{val}}$ (new data, disjoint from the Phase~0/1
    core); a diffable memory store.
  \item \textbf{Experiments.} Compare $\{$no memory, retrieval-only, distilled
    $M$ with elitism, distilled $M$ without elitism$\}$ on the held-out split; a
    pollution stress test injecting wrong priors and checking elitism rejects
    them.
  \item \textbf{Success metric.} Monotone non-decreasing held-out proxy across
    accepted updates, a positive gap of ``$M$ with elitism'' over both baselines,
    and zero acceptance of injected harmful priors.
  \item \textbf{Risk.} Belief leaking into evidence, or proxy overfitting;
    mitigate with the evidence/belief split and periodic held-out refresh.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Phase~4 --- Closing the control channel critic\,$\rightarrow$\,generator (P5).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Goal.} Implement $\delta:\mathcal{F}\mapsto\Delta_{\mathrm{ctrl}}$ of
    \eqref{eq:control}, converting a contradiction packet into a \emph{targeted}
    local edit of the MuJoCo/VACE control signal, and regenerate.
  \item \textbf{Needs.} A parameterization of Wan2.2-VACE control inputs by
    obligation type; a differentiable-or-heuristic edit policy $\delta$; a
    re-adjudication harness.
  \item \textbf{Experiments.} On clips with \contradicted{} verdicts, compare
    $\{$blind resampling, prompt-only edit, targeted $\delta(\mathcal{F})$ edit$\}$; report
    the fraction of previously violated obligations that flip to \supported{}
    after regeneration, \emph{human-verified}, and re-run the full obligation set
    $C$ post-edit to report net defect change.
  \item \textbf{Success metric.} Targeted $\delta(\mathcal{F})$ regeneration repairs a
    significantly higher fraction of localized violations than blind resampling
    at equal generation budget, confirmed by human annotation (not by the critic
    alone), with no rise in unrelated-obligation contradictions.
  \item \textbf{Risk.} Goodharting the critic or whack-a-mole edits that fix one
    obligation while breaking another; mitigated by human verification and the
    net-defect audit.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Phase~5 --- Breaking the loop with external knowledge $K$ (P6).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Goal.} Open the internal CoT$\rightarrow$feedback$\rightarrow$context
    loop by admitting external/social knowledge and retrieval into $K$ under the
    same candidate-and-pruning discipline, with a do-nothing fallback.
  \item \textbf{Needs.} A retrieval/ingestion interface; a candidate gate scored
    by $U_{\mathrm{val}}$; provenance tagging that marks $K$-sourced facts
    distinctly from distilled beliefs.
  \item \textbf{Experiments.} On a bias-probe subset (defect modes
    under-represented in the internal traces), compare closed-loop
    ($K=\emptyset$) vs.\ open-loop ($K$ admitted with gating); a noise-injection
    test on retrieved sources.
  \item \textbf{Success metric.} Reduced error on the bias-probe subset with no
    regression on the general held-out split; zero admissions when no candidate
    improves the proxy (fallback correctness).
  \item \textbf{Risk.} Context pollution from noisy or off-distribution sources;
    mitigated by the same elitism gate as $S$, plus source provenance so bad
    admissions are revertible.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Phase~6 --- Human-in-the-loop deployment at scale (P4).}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Goal.} Operate both channels under human supervision: reviewers
    audit traces $T$, veto/edit candidate state updates, and gate deployment of
    refined generations and new states $S'$.
  \item \textbf{Needs.} Trace-replay and state-diff review tooling; an approval
    workflow feeding $\mathrm{Distill}$ and $K$; audit logs over
    $S\!\rightarrow\!S'$ with rollback.
  \item \textbf{Experiments.} Held-out study measuring reviewer time per audited
    trace, inter-annotator agreement on state-update acceptance and on
    three-valued verdicts, and the catch-rate of injected bad updates that
    elitism alone would have missed.
  \item \textbf{Success metric.} Auditing cost per trace low enough to be
    practical, with human review reliably catching a measurable residual of
    harmful updates beyond the automatic gate.
  \item \textbf{Risk.} Reviewer fatigue and rubber-stamping (which would
    reintroduce an unaudited closed loop); mitigate by surfacing only
    elitism-borderline updates for human attention.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\emph{Ordering rationale.} Phase~1 secures the evaluation;
Phases~2--3 grow the two components of $S$ that the inward channel writes ($\mathcal{O}$,
$M$); Phase~4 exercises the outward channel; Phase~5 opens $K$; Phase~6 wraps
everything in human oversight. Each phase reuses the Phase~0 read path unchanged,
so at every step the system remains weight-free and auditable by construction.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{Generation.} The control signal comes from a simulator, so scene diversity is
bounded by what is simulated, and the mapping from simulation to prompt appearance is not
itself verified for physical consistency.

\paragraph{Benchmark.} The corpus is recall-only (no clean clips), so precision is
not directly measurable; flaws are single-annotator with no inter-annotator
agreement; the scope/category tags are machine-derived; and the source generator
of the clips is unrecorded. Repeatedly designing against one frozen set risks
overfitting to it even without training on it.

\paragraph{Critic.} The spatial relation predicate and negative-event
verification are limited; the typed-plan layer does not yet dispatch the full
specialist registry; binding remains a central failure mode, since the compiler
enforces symbolic identifier consistency but cannot guarantee that heterogeneous
tools localized the same physical instance; and the reasoner-mediated sub-checks
reduce but do not remove semantic opacity.

\paragraph{Self-evolution.} Four limits bound what
\cref{sec:selfevolve:measured} establishes. The acceptance gate
\eqref{eq:elitism} is not exercised, so the measurement covers distillation
without the admission discipline that is meant to keep a bad lesson out; a rule
written from a fresh miss has no proxy history, and closing that gap needs a
$U_{\mathrm{val}}$ defined on unrun rules. The taught planner reads $1.76$ times
as many claims out of a prompt as the untaught one, and flaw recall rewards
asking more, so the effect combines a better-targeted plan with a larger one; a
run holding the taught arm to the untaught arm's question budget would separate
them. The lessons are induced once from a fixed pool rather than accumulated online, so the curve of
\cref{fig:iclaggregate} measures the effect of the amount of experience distilled, not of
accumulating it during deployment; the shape beyond $117$
experience clips is unmeasured. Finally, the
$502$-flaw held-out set is a different population from the $304$-flaw evaluation
core used everywhere else in this paper, and the two rates should not be compared
directly. Every distilled prior is \emph{critic-derived} rather than ground truth,
and retrieval quality, staleness, and the propagation of a wrong prior remain
open. The operator set $\mathcal{O}$ and the external channel $K$ are untouched: growing
the operator library through multimodal
distillation~\cite{plugmem2026,vilomem2026,m3agent2025} and admitting external
knowledge under a candidate-and-prune
discipline~\cite{contexttraining2026} are future work.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% =====================================================================
Physical faithfulness in generated video requires three things that are usually
studied apart: a way to \emph{steer} what gets generated, a way to \emph{measure}
whether physical obligations are met, and \emph{ground truth} against which
measurements can be scored. This report contributes one artifact toward each. For
steering, we build a simulation-driven controllable generator
(MuJoCo\,$\rightarrow$\,Wan~2.2-VACE), a first step toward the control channel
through which a critic might one day feed corrections back into generation. For
ground truth, we assemble a benchmark of human-annotated, localized defects, so
that a critic can be scored on whether it finds the specific fault rather than on
aggregate preference. And for measurement, we present \PhyReAct{}, an auditable
multi-tool critic that grounds the reason--act--observe loop in physical
verification: reasoning compiles the prompt into typed physical checks, frozen
expert operators are called only to return measurements over the scope those
checks declare, and a deterministic roll-up folds the observations into a
three-valued verdict (\supported{}/\contradicted{}/\unknownv{}, surfaced as
\emph{plausible}, \emph{implausible}, or \abstain{}) with a human-traceable
evidence chain.

On a development core of $149$ clips carrying $304$ human-marked defects,
\PhyReAct{} accounts for $228$, against $164$ for a published question-decomposition
evaluator reading the same clips and checking the same claims with the same served
model. Prompting the same backbone monolithically reaches $222$, so the count alone does not
separate the two. Every one of \PhyReAct{}'s $228$ arrives with the number that forced it, and
a reader can audit any single verdict instead of accepting an aggregate.

That auditability is also what makes the evidence usable downstream. A contradicted
claim names what failed, when it failed, and on what measurement, which is precisely
the signal a generator needs to regenerate a clip locally rather than resample it
whole. It is equally the substrate for an operator library that grows as new
measurement procedures are distilled, and for improvement that edits a readable state
rather than a weight. Closing the critic-to-generator loop over that signal is the
next milestone.
